### PAGE 4 — The oath called in.
**PANEL 1.** [SET-MYCENAE], the megaron: [AGAMEMNON] with the herald's tablet in his fist, [MENELAUS] beside him hollow and still; scribes already writing.
> AGAMEMNON: Every king who stood at the horse-sacrifice swore to defend this marriage against any man on earth. Very well. Send to every king who swore.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** Montage: HERALDS riding out of the Lion Gate in every direction, staffs of office raised; roads diverging across the mountains.
> CAPTION: And the heralds went out to every royal hall in Greece, carrying two words: THE OATH. Some kings rose gladly, smelling plunder. Some rose grimly, keeping faith. Some looked for a way out. All rose. An oath sworn on blood does not offer a third choice.
**PANEL 3.** [AGAMEMNON] before a great hide map, moving carved ship-tokens; ambition kindling in the heavy-browed face.
> CAPTION: And Agamemnon, counting the kings who must now follow his house to war, understood what his brother's loss had bought him: the high command of all Greece. Grief is grief, princes. But watch what men build with it.

When I sing him, I sing your grandfathers' grandfather. He alone of all these kings will come home from this war with clean hands and a whole heart. Ask yourselves, as we go, how he manages it.

### PAGE 6 — The madman's plow.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-ITHACA]: the shore field below the hall — [ODYSSEUS] plowing sand with an OX and an ASS yoked together, casting SALT behind him like seed, hair wild, eyes empty; [PENELOPE] at the field's edge with the infant TELEMACHUS in her arms, her face carefully unreadable; the summons-party watching in dismay.
> CAPTION: But on Ithaca the heralds found a ruin. Odysseus — the mind that had tied the oath — met them plowing the beach with an ox and an ass yoked as a team, sowing the furrows with salt, singing to himself. A madman cannot swear. A madman cannot sail.
**PANEL 2.** Among the summons-party: [PALAMEDES], elegant, head tilted, watching the plowman with the pleasure of a craftsman studying a rival's work. (births refs/palamedes.png)
> CAPTION: With the heralds had come Palamedes of Nauplia — a lord almost as famous for cleverness as Odysseus himself. And cleverness knows cleverness, princes, the way one forger knows another's coin.
**PANEL 3.** [PALAMEDES] lifting the infant TELEMACHUS gently from Penelope's arms — her single step of protest — his courteous, terrible smile.
> PALAMEDES: A beautiful son, lady. Let him greet his father.

### PAGE 7 — The exposure.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The field: [PALAMEDES] setting the swaddled INFANT on the sand directly in the path of the oncoming plow-team; [PENELOPE]'s hand at her mouth; the heralds frozen.
> CAPTION: And he laid the child in the furrow, in the path of the team.
**PANEL 2.** [ODYSSEUS] — the plow swerving hard aside, his arm hauling the yoke over, the madness dropping from his face like a mask cut loose; the child untouched.
> CAPTION: The plow turned. Of course it turned. No man alive could have held that furrow — and so the whole beach knew that the plowman was a man alive, and sane, and caught.
**PANEL 3.** [ODYSSEUS] straightening slowly over the child, and the look he gives [PALAMEDES] — long, level, unblinking; Palamedes's answering bow, graceful and pleased.
> ODYSSEUS: ...Well solved, son of Nauplius.
> CAPTION: Three words, and courteous ones. But mark that look, princes, and enter it in the ledger. Odysseus forgave many things in his long life. Being beaten at his own art was never one of them. Palamedes has perhaps eight years to live.

### PAGE 8 — SPLASH: THE FLEET AT AULIS.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** [SET-AULIS] from the hills: the bay BLACK with beached ships rank on rank to the strait's far shore, contingent on contingent — tower shields of Salamis, Cretan double-axes, Pylian sand-colored sails, Myrmidon black hulls to come — smoke of a thousand fires, tent-lines, horse-parks, the great plane tree and the altars small at the water's edge.
> CAPTION (ornate, top): So the kings kept the oath, and Greece emptied into one bay. From rocky Ithaca and sandy Pylos, from Crete of the hundred cities, from Salamis and Argos and Tiryns of the great walls — more ships than the sea had ever carried, under one command: Agamemnon, lord of Mycenae, king of kings.
> CAPTION (ornate, bottom): The full count of the ships I will sing in its own place, as the great singer does, when they stand off Troy at last. Tonight, only know: the world had never seen the like. And it was about to sail the wrong way.

### PAGE 9 — The sign at the spring.
**PANEL 1.** The altars under the great plane tree by the spring: [AGAMEMNON] and the kings sacrificing; [CALCHAS] the seer presiding, feather-mantled, pale-eyed. (births refs/calchas.png)
> CAPTION: At the altars by the spring, under the one great plane tree, the kings made offering for the sailing. And the gods answered — but not from the fire.
**PANEL 2.** THE SIGN: out from beneath the altar, a blood-red SERPENT streaking up the plane tree toward a nest of nine — eight sparrow chicks and the mother fluttering; the kings turning, frozen mid-rite.
> CAPTION: A serpent, back blood-red, ran from beneath the altar and up the tree — to a sparrow's nest on the highest bough: eight nestlings, and the mother that hatched them made nine.
**PANEL 3.** The serpent, the nest empty, turned to STONE mid-coil on the branch; the army staring in dead silence.
> CAPTION: It devoured all nine. And the god who sent it turned it to stone where it coiled, in the sight of the whole army. Silence, princes, is the loudest sound ten thousand men can make.

### PAGE 10 — Calchas reads the years.
**PANEL 1.** [CALCHAS], arms spread, pale eyes rolled to the sign, the seer's voice upon him.
> CALCHAS: Hear it, Achaeans! Nine the serpent swallowed — nine years shall you war and win nothing. But in the TENTH year the wide-wayed city falls. Zeus of the counsels has shown it: late-fulfilled, but its fame shall never die!
**PANEL 2.** The kings' faces down the line: awe, dismay, resolve — [AGAMEMNON] hearing "the city falls" and nothing else; [ODYSSEUS] hearing "nine years" and everything else; old [NESTOR] simply nodding, as at a fair price named.
> CAPTION: Ten years. Watch each king hear it, and you know each king.
**PANEL 3.** [ODYSSEUS], quietly, to Nestor, the stone serpent behind them.
> ODYSSEUS: Remember this sign with me, old king. There will come a day, deep in those nine years, when this whole army will break for the ships and home — and one of us will have to stand in their way and remind them what the god promised. Remember it with me.
> CAPTION: Enter it in the ledger, princes: a promise of Zeus, and a man saving it for the hour of despair. You will see him spend it.

### PAGE 11 — The guideless sea.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The armada standing out from Aulis in fair wind, glorious.
> CAPTION: So they sailed — a thousand ships, ten thousand spears, and not one man aboard who knew the way to Troy.
**PANEL 2.** On the flagship: kings arguing over a crude hide chart; pilots shrugging; [AGAMEMNON] impatient, jabbing eastward.
> CAPTION: Understand it, princes: the sea-roads east were traders' secrets, and no trader sat on that flagship. They knew Troy lay east, and that Asia was wide. It was enough for pride. It is never enough for war.
**PANEL 3.** A foreign coast rising ahead at dusk: walled towns, river-mouth, rich fields — Mysia.
> CAPTION: And when a rich coast rose against the evening — walls, herds, a river — Agamemnon looked at it and saw what he had come to see. "Troy," said the king of kings. It was not Troy.

### PAGE 12 — The wrong war.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The beach assault at dawn: Achaeans storming ashore into Mysia, torching boats and granaries, driving herds; defenders scrambling; war in full ugly flood — and every banner on the walls not Trojan.
> CAPTION: They fell on Teuthrania of the Mysians as on Troy — beach and granary and town. The Mysians had stolen no queen and sworn no oath. They fought because armed strangers were burning their homes. There is a lesson here so plain I will not insult you by speaking it.
**PANEL 2.** Down the beach at a run with the Myrmidon spearhead: a YOUNG WARRIOR outstripping everyone, red-gold hair streaming from under his helmet, terrible and beautiful and laughing — [ACHILLES], with steady [PATROCLUS] guarding his unshielded side. (births refs/achilles.png, refs/patroclus.png)
> CAPTION: And here, princes — in the wrong war, on the wrong beach — the song first shows you its greatest name. Achilles, son of Peleus and the sea-goddess Thetis: sixteen years old, faster than the rest of mankind, and already worth half the army. Beside him, as always, from now until the hour that breaks the world: Patroclus.
**PANEL 3.** The defenders' line rallying around a TAWNY-BEARDED KING of lion-like power laying about him — [TELEPHUS], son of Heracles. (births refs/telephus.png)
> CAPTION: But Mysia had a king worth an army too: Telephus — a son of Heracles himself. Yes, princes: the blood of the hero of the first song, standing against the Greeks. The old threads never leave this loom.

### PAGE 13 — The wounding.
**PANEL 1.** [TELEPHUS] turning the Achaean tide almost alone, a Greek lord fallen before him (Thersander of Thebes, dying with dignity, no gore).
> CAPTION: Telephus killed Thersander of Thebes, a king in his own right, and rolled the whole landing back toward the sea. The blood of Heracles was paying for its ground.
**PANEL 2.** The turn: [TELEPHUS] in mid-stride tripped — his ankle caught in a VINE that has whipped up out of the trampled earth itself, unnatural, deliberate; his arms flung out.
> CAPTION: Then the earth itself betrayed him: a vine — a vine, on a war-beach — took his ankle like a snare. The vine belongs to Dionysus, whose rites Telephus had once slighted. No debt sleeps forever, princes; some merely wait for the worst possible moment. That is rather their point.
**PANEL 3.** [ACHILLES] on him in one bound — the great ASH SPEAR OF PELION taking the fallen king through the thigh; the spear's first blood; [PATROCLUS] arriving a step behind.
> CAPTION: And Achilles was on him, and the ash spear of Pelion — Chiron's wedding-gift, that only he could lift — drank its first blood: the thigh of a son of Heracles. Mark the wound. The whole war will hang from it before we are done tonight.

### PAGE 14 — The error known.
**PANEL 1.** The fighting dying; a captured Mysian ELDER dragged before [AGAMEMNON] and the kings, shouting one word again and again, pointing north-east; [ODYSSEUS]'s face going flat as he understands first.
> CAPTION: Then a grey prisoner was dragged before the kings, and he was not crying for mercy. He was crying a name. "MYSIA," he cried. "This is MYSIA, madmen. Troy is NORTH."
**PANEL 2.** The kings on the ravaged beach among their own dead and the smoke: shame settling over them like weather; [MENELAUS] turning away; [NESTOR] old and grim; [AGAMEMNON] refusing to lower his eyes, which is its own confession.
> CAPTION: So the first blood of the great war for the oath of Tyndareus was Mysian blood, owed by no one, shed for nothing. The kings stood among the burning boats of strangers and understood what they had done. Some wars begin with a crime, princes. This one managed to begin with a blunder AND a crime, and had not yet seen Troy.
**PANEL 3.** The fleets pulling off the hostile shore in disorder under a bruising sky.
> CAPTION: They re-embarked in shame — and the sky, which had watched it all, decided it had seen enough.

### PAGE 15 — The scattering.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The great storm: the armada torn apart across a black sea, ships driven over three horizons, lightning walking on the water.
> CAPTION: The storm took the thousand ships and dealt them like a thrown deck across the whole Aegean. The first mustering of the Greeks ended with no two kings in sight of one another.
**PANEL 2.** Fragments: a Pylian ship running home; Cretans bailing; Agamemnon's flagship alone in a grey waste.
> CAPTION: They limped home singly, king by king, over weeks — to wives who asked how the war had gone, a question with no bearable answer.
**PANEL 3.** One black ship driven toward a white-rocked island rising from blue water: [SET-SCYROS].
> CAPTION: And one storm-thread the gods wove with particular care: the ship of Achilles they drove to the island of Scyros. Nothing in this song is only weather, princes.

### PAGE 16 — Scyros.
**PANEL 1.** [SET-SCYROS]: the small bright palace; old King Lycomedes welcoming the storm-cast [ACHILLES] and [PATROCLUS] under guest-law; and among the household, a sweet-faced princess looking up — [DEIDAMIA]. (births refs/deidamia.png)
> CAPTION: King Lycomedes of Scyros took the shipwrecked heroes in, as guest-law commands. He had a daughter, Deidamia.
**PANEL 2.** The waiting months, one warm panel: [ACHILLES] and [DEIDAMIA] on the palace terrace above the bay, close and glad; [PATROCLUS] below on the sand, drilling the crews, giving them the time.
> CAPTION: The ships needed a season's mending, and the season was kind. Before it ended, Achilles and Deidamia were wed. It was — hear this and keep it, princes — the one purely happy thing that ever happened to that shining and doomed young man. One island season. The gods do not give him another.
**PANEL 3.** The leave-taking at the shore: [DEIDAMIA] with her hand at her waist in the ancient gesture; [ACHILLES] holding her face; the ships waiting.
> CAPTION: And when the summons found him and he sailed, Deidamia carried his child. Enter the ledger's largest entry, princes: the son of Achilles — Neoptolemus, "new-war." He will be born after his father sails, grow up on this island, and come to Troy only when... no. That is the end of the song, and this is its beginning. But remember Scyros.
> FOOTNOTE: *Some later singers tell of Achilles hidden on Scyros among the king's daughters in a girl's dress, found out by a trick of Odysseus. The old song of the Cycle knows only the storm and the marriage, and I sing the old song.

### PAGE 17 — The frame weighs the false war. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The Miletus hall; the NELEID-PRINCE, troubled in a new way.
> NELEID-PRINCE: Father — the Mysian dead. Who pays for THEM? They were no part of the oath, the theft, the plan. Does heaven's ledger have a page for the wrong country?
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, and for once he is slow to answer.
> SINGER: ...That, child, is the best question anyone has asked me in this hall, and I will not cheapen it with a neat answer. The songs are silent on the Mysian dead. Perhaps the silence is the answer. Perhaps the silence is the wound. When you are a king, remember that the songs go silent exactly where kings most need them to speak.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER, gathering the thread again.
> SINGER: But one Mysian the song does follow — because his wound would not heal.

### PAGE 18 — The wound that would not heal.
**PANEL 1.** Mysia: [TELEPHUS] on his throne, aged by pain, the thigh-wound bound and black-veined, physicians helpless around him.
> CAPTION: The spear-wound of Achilles festered in Telephus year after year, past every herb and every prayer — for it was no common spear, and heaven does not let its instruments heal at random.
**PANEL 2.** The oracle-cave: [TELEPHUS] before a smoking cleft, receiving the riddle.
> CAPTION: Until the god at last gave him a riddle for his pain: THE WOUNDER SHALL HEAL. Nothing else. Gods bill by the word, princes, and every word costs a journey.
**PANEL 3.** [TELEPHUS] wrapped as a ragged suppliant, boarding a small ship alone, the lion-claw clasp hidden at his throat.
> CAPTION: So the son of Heracles wrapped himself in a beggar's cloak and sailed — alone, in pain, into the country of his enemies — to ask the man who ruined him to mend him. Weigh the courage of that, princes, against all the shield-glory in this song. I know where I set my own weight.

### PAGE 19 — SPLASH: THE SUPPLIANT AT THE HEARTH. *(Tier C, flagged — the Orestes device)*
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The megaron of Mycenae at night: [TELEPHUS], ragged, wild, kneeling ON the great central hearth itself in the suppliant's last refuge — and held gently in his arms, lifted from his cradle, the infant ORESTES, son of Agamemnon, the suppliant's living shield; before him the frozen half-circle: [AGAMEMNON] with a hand half-raised and stopped, [MENELAUS], [ODYSSEUS] watchful, [NESTOR] grave, guards helpless; [CLYTEMNESTRA] at the chamber door, utterly still, her eyes not on Telephus but on her husband's half-raised hand.
> CAPTION (ornate, top): He came to Mycenae as a beggar, and when the guards would have taken him, he did the one thing no Greek hand could answer: he took up the king's own infant son, Orestes, and knelt with him upon the holy hearth — suppliant of the hearth, holding the house's heir. Now every law of heaven stood armed around him.
> CAPTION (ornate, bottom): Look long at this picture, princes; the loom is showing its threads. The baby in the suppliant's arms will one day avenge this house. The woman in the doorway, watching her husband's raised hand, is learning tonight how that hand weighs a child's life against a king's need. She will have cause to remember. And the ragged man on the hearth is the son of Heracles, teaching the whole heroic world its own deepest law: that the helpless suppliant is stronger than the armed king. File it all away. The song forgets nothing.

### PAGE 20 — The wounder shall heal.
**PANEL 1.** The council: [TELEPHUS]'s claim laid out; [ACHILLES] summoned, standing puzzled and young before the wounded king.
> ACHILLES: I am no physician. I never learned the healing herbs — Chiron taught me spears, not simples. How can I heal what I cannot even name?
**PANEL 2.** [ODYSSEUS], one finger raised, the pleasure of the solving in his grey eyes.
> ODYSSEUS: Because the oracle never named YOU, son of Peleus. "The wounder shall heal." What wounded him? Not the man. The SPEAR. Bring the ash of Pelion — and a scraping-blade.
**PANEL 3.** The healing: rust and filings scraped from the great spearhead into the open wound; [TELEPHUS]'s face unclenching for the first time in years; [CALCHAS] confirming with spread hands; the kings exhaling.
> CAPTION: They scraped the rust of the great spear into the wound — and the wound that no herb could touch began to close before their eyes. The wounder healed. Write the rule on your hearts, princes, for this song will use it again and again until the very end: WHAT HARMS IS WHAT CURES, and the road back always runs through the thing itself. Remember the spear-rust when you meet a wooden horse.

### PAGE 21 — The bargain of Telephus.
**PANEL 1.** [TELEPHUS] healed, standing straight, before the assembled kings: the price named.
> AGAMEMNON: You have your healing, Mysian. Now the fee: the sea-road to Troy. You know it. Sail with us and pilot the fleet.
**PANEL 2.** [TELEPHUS], quiet iron.
> TELEPHUS: I will guide you to Troy's shore — that, for my healing. But I will not draw spear against the city. Priam's own sister is my wife, Greeks; my son Eurypylus is half of Troy's blood. A man does not war on his wife's kin. Guide, yes. Fight, never.
**PANEL 3.** The kings accepting; and the singer's aside on the boy across the sea — a small inset: a young boy on a far Anatolian terrace.
> CAPTION: And that was honorably said, and honorably kept. But enter the son in the ledger, princes: Eurypylus, half Heracles and half Troy. Years from now, when Troy is desperate and Achilles is dust, that boy — grown terrible — will be bought to Troy's wall by a gift of a golden vine, and the son of Achilles will meet him there. The spear that healed the father will not spare the son. The loom is patient.

### PAGE 22 — The years between.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Montage panel, divided light: ships drawn up under sheds through rains and summers; forges glowing; boys drilling with spears who were infants at the first muster.
> CAPTION: Then the years passed — for a muster broken must be gathered again, and kingdoms bled by a false war must refill. The songs count differently; count with me simply: years, while the oath lay banked like a covered fire.
**PANEL 2.** [SET-ITHACA]: [PENELOPE] on the shore with TELEMACHUS now a small boy running ahead of her; [ODYSSEUS] watching them both from the hall door, memorizing.
> CAPTION: On Ithaca, Telemachus learned to run on the beach where his father had plowed salt. Odysseus watched his son grow and his olive-tree bed stand rooted, and knew to the day how much happiness he was being allowed to bank. Clever men suffer worst, princes: they see the bill coming.
**PANEL 3.** Troy: [HELEN] at a great loom in an upper room, weaving — and in the web, small, discernible: battles, ships, burning. [PARIS-prince] behind her, not looking at the web.
> CAPTION: And in Troy, Helen of Sparta — Helen of Troy now, as the world was learning to say — sat at her loom and wove. And what she wove, the great singer will tell you when he finds her at it years hence: the battles of Trojans and Greeks, fought for her sake. She was weaving this song, princes, before most of it had happened. Some knowledge needs no seer's fillet. Some knowledge is just a mirror.

### PAGE 23 — The second summons.
**PANEL 1.** [SET-MYCENAE]: [AGAMEMNON] older, harder, before the assembled heralds once more; the map hide worn smooth at Troy.
> AGAMEMNON: Telephus knows the road. The ships are new-tarred, the boys are grown to spearmen. Send to every king a second time: AULIS. And this time, no man's plow and no man's madness excuses him.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** The converging roads again, mirrored from page 4 but grimmer: columns of spears on every pass, no garlands now, veteran faces.
> CAPTION: And Greece rose the second time — heavier, grimmer, better. The first muster had been a festival with weapons. This one was a war.
**PANEL 3.** Contingent-arrival montage at Aulis: the tower shield of AJAX planting like a door; DIOMEDES's boar-blazon; IDOMENEUS's double axe; the bay filling again.
> CAPTION: Ajax of Salamis, a wall that walked. Diomedes of Argos, keen as a whetted edge. Idomeneus of Crete, grave and sure. The suitors of the wedding-court, princes — come to pay the wedding's bill.

### PAGE 24 — The Myrmidons.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The black ships of Phthia coming into Aulis under sail, fifty of them, crews rowing in dead-even silence; the whole beach turning to watch.
> CAPTION: And last came fifty black ships from Phthia, rowed in perfect time and perfect silence, and the whole armada stopped its noise to watch them beach. The Myrmidons had come.
**PANEL 2.** [ACHILLES] leaping the gunwale to the sand before the ship touches, no longer a boy — the finished weapon, beautiful and appalling; [PATROCLUS] a stride behind; old PHOENIX at the rail.
> CAPTION: Achilles came down the beach, and grown kings who had sacked cities felt their throats go dry. He was worth ships beyond counting, and every man there knew it — none better than the king of kings, who owned the command but not the flame. File THAT away too, princes. It is the seed of the greatest song of all.
**PANEL 3.** Beyond the surf-line, unnoticed: a woman's shape in the standing swell, silver-dark, watching the beach — [THETIS], her immortal face grieving.
> CAPTION: And out past the surf, where no one looked, the sea stood a little taller than the sea. Thetis watched her son walk up the sand into his short forever. She knew the two fates over him — the long dim life, the brief blazing one — and she knew which one walks off a ship at Aulis.

### PAGE 25 — Troy hears.
**PANEL 1.** [SET-WILUSA-COURT]: a coast-watcher's report; [PRIAM] older; [HECTOR] rising from the bench, taking the report from the messenger's hand himself.
> CAPTION: And Troy heard. This time there was no error in the sea-roads and no doubt in the reports: the greatest fleet in the world's history was massing, and its heading was the Hellespont.
**PANEL 2.** [HECTOR] before the council, calm, thorough, already commanding: pointing at the wall-map — shore watch, granaries, wells, the allies.
> HECTOR: Grain into the citadel. The springs walled. Watch-fires the length of the coast. And heralds to every ally from the Axius to Lycia — Troy calls in her own oaths now. If they come, they will find no sleeping city.
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM] watching his eldest son organize the survival of the city, pride and dread together; through the window, Ida serene.
> CAPTION: And Priam watched Hector — thirty years old, and already the best man in this entire song; yes, princes, I said what I said, and I will stand behind it at the end — and the old king thought: whatever my second son has broken, my first may yet hold. Half of that thought will prove true.

### PAGE 26 — Olympus tallies. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
