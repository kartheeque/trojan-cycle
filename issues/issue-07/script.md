### PAGE 4 — The refusal.
**PANEL 1.** The assembly-ground: [CHRYSES] before the enthroned kings, staff and scarlet fillets high, the ransom-chest open at his feet.
> CHRYSES: Sons of Atreus, bronze-greaved Achaeans — may the gods grant you Priam's city, and a safe sailing home! Only give me back my child, and take this ransom — and honor the son of Zeus, Apollo who strikes from far away.
**PANEL 2.** The army's murmur of assent rolling around the ring — every face but one saying yes; the ransom is rich, the fillets are holy.
> CAPTION: And the whole army rumbled its assent: honor the priest, take the shining price. The whole army — but not the man whose prize she was.
**PANEL 3.** [AGAMEMNON] rising, and the refusal like a lash; the old man flinching back a step.
> AGAMEMNON: Let me not find you at the ships again, old man — lingering now or creeping back later — or the god's staff and ribbons will be no shield to you! The girl I will NOT release. Old age will find her in MY house, in Argos, far from her country, at the loom and at my bed's service. Go. Do not provoke me. Go while you can go safely.
> CAPTION: So he spoke. And the old man was afraid, and obeyed.

### PAGE 5 — The prayer.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [CHRYSES] walking away small and silent along the shore of the tumbling sea, the camp's fires behind him.
> CAPTION: And Chryses went in silence along the shore of the loud-roaring sea. And when he had gone far apart, the old man prayed — to the lord his whole life had served.
**PANEL 2.** The prayer: [CHRYSES] with arms lifted to the darkening sky, the fillets streaming.
> CHRYSES: Hear me, god of the silver bow — Smintheus*, lord of Chryse! If ever I roofed a shrine that pleased you — if ever I burned you the fat thighs of bulls and goats — grant me this one thing: LET THE GREEKS PAY FOR MY TEARS UNDER YOUR ARROWS.
> FOOTNOTE: *SMINTHEUS — "Mouse-lord," an ancient title of Apollo in these coastlands, master of the plague he sends and lifts.
**PANEL 3.** Silence after; the old man lowering his arms; and far behind the clouds, a brightness beginning to move.
> CAPTION: So he prayed. And Phoebus Apollo heard him.

### PAGE 6 — The arrows of the god.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** APOLLO descending from the peaks of Olympus — rendered as Homer gives it: a radiant terror coming down LIKE THE NIGHT, the silver bow across his back, the arrows clanging in the quiver at his shoulder with every stride of his anger; the camp small below.
> CAPTION: Down from Olympus he came with anger in his heart, the bow and the hooded quiver on his shoulders — and the arrows rattled on the shoulders of the angry god as he moved. HE CAME LIKE THE NIGHT.
**PANEL 2.** The first days: the god kneeling on a dune apart from the ships, loosing shaft after shaft of pale fire — and in the camp, mules and dogs stricken first, then here and there a man falling at his cook-fire.
> CAPTION: He knelt apart from the ships and let fly. First the mules he struck, and the swift dogs; then the men themselves — and the arrow-fall never slackened, day upon day.
**PANEL 3.** The ninth day: the shoreline dense with FUNERAL PYRES burning, smoke staining the whole sky; the living moving like shadows between them.
> CAPTION: For nine days the god's arrows fell on the army. And the pyres of the dead burned thick along the shore, always, without end.

### PAGE 7 — The assembly.
**PANEL 1.** The tenth day: the army assembled; [ACHILLES] standing to speak — for it was Achilles, not the king, whom Hera moved to act.
> ACHILLES: Son of Atreus — if war and plague together are to reap us, the survivors had best sail home! But first: let us ask some seer or priest — some reader of dreams — WHY Apollo rages. What vow, what offering, has gone unpaid? Let us learn how to turn this ruin aside.
**PANEL 2.** [CALCHAS] rising, pale, and the condition before the truth.
> CALCHAS: Achilles, dear to Zeus — you ask me to name the anger of the god. I will name it. But swear first to defend me, with word and hand — for what I say will enrage a great man, a king over Argives. And a king's grudge against a lesser man may sleep a day; it does not die until it is paid.
**PANEL 3.** [ACHILLES]'s pledge, instant and absolute, his hand out; and his eyes already going to Agamemnon.
> ACHILLES: Take heart and speak, whatever you have seen. While I live and look on this earth, no man of all this army lays a heavy hand on you by the hollow ships — NO man. Not even if you name... Agamemnon.
> CAPTION: The name was not yet an accusation. It was already an aim.

### PAGE 8 — SPLASH: THE QUARREL, AND THE HAND OF ATHENA.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The full assembly at maximum voltage, one frozen instant: [AGAMEMNON] on his feet, scepter leveled, face black with fury, his threat just spoken; opposite him [ACHILLES] mid-motion — the great sword HALF-DRAWN from its sheath, his whole body a single blasphemous intention — and behind him, VISIBLE TO HIM AND THE READER ALONE, [ATHENA] manifest at half-divine scale, her hand closed in his red-gold hair, hauling his head back, her grey eyes blazing an inch from his; the ringed army seeing only a man hesitating strangely at his sword-hilt; [NESTOR], [ODYSSEUS], the kings frozen in the ring.
> CAPTION (ornate, top): Calchas spoke: the god rages for his priest — return the girl, unbought, and the plague ends. And the quarrel came down like a mountain: the king would yield his prize only if given Achilles's own — Briseis. Word struck word like flint on flint, until the son of Peleus's hand was on his sword and the king of men was one heartbeat from death in front of his own army —
> CAPTION (ornate, bottom): — and Athena came down from heaven, sent by white-armed Hera who loved both men, and stood behind Achilles and seized him by the hair, seen by him alone. And the moment held, princes: the whole war, the whole song, the whole world balanced on one half-drawn sword and one goddess's grip. "I have come to stop your fury — IF you will obey," said the grey-eyed one. "Sheathe the sword. Wound him with words as you please; heaven will owe you three times these gifts. But sheathe the sword." And Achilles — hear the exact measure of the man — obeyed. The heavy hand stayed on the silver hilt. And the sword went back.

### PAGE 9 — The oath on the scepter.
**PANEL 1.** [ACHILLES], sword sheathed, taking up instead the assembly's great SCEPTER — and the words worse than any sword.
> ACHILLES: Wine-heavy king — dog's eyes and deer's heart! You never arm with the men, you never lie in ambush with the best of us — you find it safer to stay in camp and confiscate the prize of any man who crosses you. A king who devours his own people! Hear my oath —
**PANEL 2.** THE OATH: Achilles holding the scepter high, gold studs catching the light.
> ACHILLES: By this scepter — which will never again grow leaf or twig, since first the axe stripped it on the mountain, and now the sons of the Achaeans carry it in judgment — as surely as this dead wood will never green again: THE DAY IS COMING when every Achaean will long for Achilles, and you, king, will tear your heart that you dishonored the best of us, while Hector cuts your people down by the shipsterns. AND ON THAT DAY I WILL NOT COME.
**PANEL 3.** The scepter FLUNG down ringing on the stones between them; the assembly's gasp; [NESTOR] rising, honey-voiced, old hands out — and the panel already telling us it is useless.
> CAPTION: And he dashed the scepter to the ground and sat. Old Nestor rose — Nestor of the honeyed voice, who had counseled two generations of better men than these — and poured his wisdom over both burning kings. Weigh his words at leisure, princes; the song records that neither man took them. Wisdom was never the shortage in this army. Obedience to it was.

### PAGE 10 — The takings.
**PANEL 1.** The shore: [CHRYSEIS] embarked on a fast black ship with a hecatomb of oxen, [ODYSSEUS] commanding the return; sails set for Chryse.
> CAPTION: So it was done as the god required: a swift ship bore Chryseis home, with a hundred beasts for the altar, and Odysseus of the many devices in command. That debt, at least, sailed toward payment.
**PANEL 2.** The huts of the Myrmidons: the two HERALDS of Agamemnon standing wretched and afraid before [ACHILLES] — who spares them with terrible courtesy; and [PATROCLUS], at Achilles's word, going inside.
> ACHILLES: Come nearer, heralds. You are not to blame — no fault of yours stands in my ledger; only his who sent you. Patroclus... bring her out. Let them take her. But let these two witness, before gods and men: when the day comes that the army needs me to keep ruin from the ships — I will not answer.
**PANEL 3.** The taking itself: [PATROCLUS] leading [BRISEIS] out by the hand and giving her to the heralds — and the woman going UNWILLINGLY, her face turned back; Patroclus unable to meet her eyes.
> CAPTION: And Patroclus led out Briseis of the lovely cheeks, and gave her to the heralds, and the woman went with them against her will. Feel the full turn of the loom's blade, princes: the gentle man who promised her a future — mark him now, handing her away and unable to look at her. The songs put the knife in quietly. It is still a knife.

### PAGE 11 — The sea answers.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [ACHILLES] apart from all his people, sitting on the shore of the grey salt water, weeping openly, looking out over the boundless deep; his arms out over the waves.
> ACHILLES: Mother! Mother — since you bore me to so SHORT a life, honor at least was owed me from Olympus — and I have none! The wide-ruling king has taken my prize with his own hands and keeps her!
> CAPTION: And the hero wept — for they wept, princes, the men of that age, greatly and without shame, as they did all things. And deep in the salt gulfs, sitting beside her ancient father, his mother heard him.
**PANEL 2.** [THETIS] rising from the grey water LIKE A MIST, and settling beside her son, her hand on his bowed head.
> THETIS: Child, why do you weep? What grief has reached your heart? Speak it — hide nothing — so that both of us may know.
**PANEL 3.** The request and the terrible arithmetic: [ACHILLES]'s tear-streaked face lifted to hers; her immortal face already grieving past what he asks.
> ACHILLES: Go to Olympus, mother. Clasp the knees of Zeus. Ask him this: let the Trojans WIN — let the Achaeans be penned dying against their own sterns — until they have their fill of their king, and the wide-ruling son of Atreus learns his madness: that he dishonored the best of the Achaeans.
> THETIS: ...Oh my child. My sorrow. Why did I raise you at all — doomed as you are to a life brief beyond all men's, and now grief-stricken beyond all men in the little of it you have? I will go. Zeus feasts far south with the blameless Ethiopians twelve days more. On the twelfth, I will go to the bronze threshold — and I will not be refused.

### PAGE 12 — The god appeased.
**PANEL 1.** Chryse: [CHRYSES] receiving [CHRYSEIS] into his arms on the altar-steps, the old man's joy total; the hecatomb ranged; [ODYSSEUS] formal and correct.
> CAPTION: At Chryse the ship came in, and Odysseus led the girl to the altar and gave her into her father's arms. And the old man rejoiced — as men rejoice, princes, who did not truly believe the gods would manage it.
**PANEL 2.** The rite entire: the hecatomb smoking to heaven; the young men singing the paean the whole day long; the god listening.
> CHRYSES: Hear me again, silver-bow! You honored my tears and struck the Achaeans hard. Now grant the second prayer as the first: TURN THE PLAGUE ASIDE.
**PANEL 3.** The camp: the arrow-fall ceased; men standing in morning light looking at their own unburned living hands; the last pyre-smoke thinning out over a clean sea.
> CAPTION: And Apollo heard, and was appeased, and the arrows ceased. So the plague ended as it began — at an old man's word. Never once in this whole song, princes, will power listen to weakness in time. It listens afterward. Afterward is the most expensive word in the language of kings.

### PAGE 13 — The nod.
**PANEL 1.** The twelfth dawn: [THETIS] rising from the sea with the mist and ascending to [SET-OLYMPUS]; finding [ZEUS] seated apart on the highest peak.
> CAPTION: And on the twelfth dawn Thetis rose from the sea with the morning mist and went up to broad heaven, and found the far-seeing son of Cronos sitting apart from all the gods on the topmost peak.
**PANEL 2.** THE SUPPLICATION: [THETIS] down before him in the full ancient posture — left arm around his knees, right hand raised to touch his chin — the sea-goddess a suppliant.
> THETIS: Father Zeus — if ever among the immortals I served you by word or deed, grant me this: HONOR MY SON, whose life is briefer than all men's, whom the lord of men has now dishonored. Give the Trojans victory — until the Achaeans give my child his honor back, and swell it. Bow your head to me, or refuse me openly — and I will know how little I count in heaven.
**PANEL 3.** The long silence; Zeus's trouble named aloud (Hera); AND THEN — THE NOD: the dark brows bending, the ambrosial hair falling forward — and ALL OLYMPUS SHAKING to its roots.
> ZEUS: ...A cursed business. You set me at war with Hera, whose tongue already spares me nothing. Go — before she sees you. But watch:
> CAPTION: And the son of Cronos bowed his dark brows; and the deathless hair streamed forward from the sovereign head; AND HE MADE GREAT OLYMPUS TREMBLE. The nod that cannot be recalled, princes. From this panel to the last page of the last song, everything now runs downhill.

### PAGE 14 — The peacemaker with the cup.
**PANEL 1.** The hall of the gods at evening: [HERA], who has seen everything, at her husband with honed words; [ZEUS]'s thunder-browed warning back; the divine feast curdling; gods staring at their plates.
> HERA: Who was scheming with you NOW, deceiver? Always your private counsels — never one willing word to ME —
> ZEUS: Hope to know all my counsels, wife, and you hope for what even you cannot carry. What is fit to hear, you hear first of any. What I purpose apart — DO NOT probe it, and do not press me, lest all Olympus not avail you when I come near.
**PANEL 2.** [HEPHAESTUS] heaving up — the lame god with the golden crutch-staff — bustling with the great two-handled cup, pouring for his mother first, deliberately comic, deliberately kind. (births refs/hephaestus.png)
> HEPHAESTUS: Mother — bear it, swallow it, dear as you are to me — or must I watch you thrashed and be able to do NOTHING? A grim feast this will be, if you two quarrel over mortals! Come — take the cup — patience — remember when I took your part before, and father seized my foot and threw me from the doorsill of heaven? A whole day I fell! Take the cup, mother.
**PANEL 3.** AND UNQUENCHABLE LAUGHTER: the whole hall of the gods breaking up at the sight of the lame god bustling with the wine — Hera smiling despite herself and taking the cup — the crisis dissolved in divine laughter; and beyond the feast, Apollo's lyre and the Muses answering one another till sundown.
> CAPTION: And laughter unquenchable rose among the blessed gods as they watched Hephaestus puffing up and down the hall. So heaven dined, and made music, and slept — while below on the dark shore ten thousand pyre-scars marked the beach, and one man sat apart by the sea, eating his own heart. Hold the two pictures together, princes. Homer holds them together. It is the coldest joint in the whole song, and he never once presses on it. He just leaves it where you must walk past.

### PAGE 15 — The frame weighs the quarrel. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The hall; the NELEID-PRINCE, hot.
> NELEID-PRINCE: The king was WRONG, father. Surely the song says the king was wrong — hoarding, grasping, shaming the best man over a prize!
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, refusing the easy coin.
> SINGER: The king was wrong first and worst, child — the song is clear, and Agamemnon himself will say so before it ends. But hear how the age reckoned: a man's prize was not his loot, it was his HONOR made visible — his timē* — the army's public measure of his worth. To seize it was to unsay his worth before ten thousand witnesses. That is why the sword came out. And now weigh Achilles: to avenge that insult he has prayed RUIN onto the whole army — onto the very men whose deaths he swore by. Right against right, princes, curdling into wrong against wrong. That is not a flaw in the song. That is the song.
> FOOTNOTE: *TIMĒ — honor as public standing: the visible price the world sets on a man. In that age, more real than gold and dearer than safety.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER, hand rising toward the vision again.
> SINGER: Now Zeus keeps the promise the nod made. And he begins — as he so often begins — with a lie.

### PAGE 16 — The false dream.
**PANEL 1.** Night camp; the DREAM — a figure of smoke and starlight in the shape of old Nestor — bending over sleeping [AGAMEMNON]'s bed.
> CAPTION: For Zeus pondered how to honor Achilles and pile the Achaeans low beside their ships — and sent baneful DREAM to the king of men, wearing the face he trusted most: Nestor's.
> DREAM: You sleep, son of wise Atreus? A man of counsels must not sleep the whole night through. Hear Zeus's word: ARM THE ARMY WITH ALL SPEED — for NOW you shall take the wide-wayed city of Troy! The immortals are of one mind at last. Hold this in your heart.
**PANEL 2.** Dawn: [AGAMEMNON] waking already aflame with it, dressing in his finest, taking up the ancestral scepter — believing.
> CAPTION: And the king woke with the divine voice still pouring round him, and believed — fool — that Troy would fall that day. He did not know the mind of Zeus, nor the sorrows yet to be piled on both sides through the great quarrel. He put on his softest tunic, and his great cloak, and took up the scepter of his fathers — deathless work — and went out to ruin his own morning.
**PANEL 3.** The council of elders first: Agamemnon recounting the dream — and proposing his disastrous cleverness: to TEST the army first with the offer of home.
> AGAMEMNON: The dream is from Zeus: the city falls today. But first — custom and prudence — I will TEST them. I will stand up and bid them flee home in the ships... and you, each from your place, will shout them back to their duty.
> CAPTION: Mark the plan, princes: tell a hundred thousand exhausted men, in the tenth year, that they may go home — as a TEST. There are wise men in every age who mistake a lever for a toy. It is about to come off the fulcrum in his hand.

### PAGE 17 — The rout for the ships.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** THE BREAK: the whole assembly detonating for the ships at the word "home" — the army moving LIKE HIGH SEA-WAVES UNDER STORM, LIKE DEEP WHEAT FLATTENED UNDER THE WEST WIND — dust to heaven, props knocked from hulls, launching-channels dug with bare hands, the cry "HOME! HOME!" made visible.
> CAPTION: He said "let us flee with our ships to our own dear country" — and stopped speaking, and the assembly rose like the sea under a squall, like a wheatfield laid flat by the west wind, and RAN. Ten years of longing went off like dry tinder. They were knocking the props from the hulls before the echo died. And the homecoming would have come, princes — beyond fate itself — had Hera not spoken to Athena, and Athena dropped like a diving hawk to one man.
**PANEL 2.** [ATHENA] at [ODYSSEUS]'s shoulder — he alone standing still in the flood, his own ship untouched, grief and fury on his face; her word like a goad.
> ATHENA: Son of Laertes — will you all truly tumble into your ships and run — and leave Priam his triumph, and leave HELEN, for whom so many Achaeans died far from home? Go at them, resourceful one. Use that voice. HOLD THEM.
**PANEL 3.** [ODYSSEUS] moving — cloak flung off, and — mark it — taking the ancestral SCEPTER out of Agamemnon's own stunned hand — and wading into the rout: gentle words to kings, the scepter's crack across the backs of bawling commoners; the flood slowing around him.
> CAPTION: And Odysseus took the scepter from the king's own hand — read that gesture fully, princes — and met every prince with honey and every bawler with the stick, and alone, man by man and rank by rank, he turned the Achaean sea back into an army.

### PAGE 18 — Thersites, and the omen spent.
**PANEL 1.** The re-formed assembly — and one voice still jeering: THERSITES, the ugliest man of the host — bandy-legged, lame, hunch-shouldered, sparse wool on his skull — railing at Agamemnon with the mob's own suppressed grievances.
> THERSITES: What's the complaint NOW, son of Atreus? Huts stuffed with bronze! First pick of the women — OUR captures! Or is it more gold you want, some Trojan father's ransom for a son WE took? Sail home, friends, and leave him to digest his prizes alone —!
> CAPTION: And here is the bitterest joke in the book, princes: crooked Thersites, whom the song is careful to make hideous, says almost exactly what ACHILLES said five days ago. From the golden mouth it was tragedy. From the ugly one it is treason. Measure that, and know the heroic world to its floor.
**PANEL 2.** [ODYSSEUS] on him: the scepter's stroke across his back, the sudden squall of tears, the whole army — moments from mutiny — ROARING with laughter at him; order restored on the hinge of one humiliation.
> CAPTION: Odysseus cracked the scepter across his back, and Thersites doubled and wept, and the army that had been mutiny itself an hour before laughed until the benches shook — and was, by that laughter, an army again. It is not a pretty mechanism, princes. The song does not claim it is pretty. It claims it is true.
**PANEL 3.** THE OMEN SPENT: [ODYSSEUS] with the scepter, now speaking to all — and above the assembly, rendered in the air of memory, the SERPENT AND THE NINE SPARROWS and the stone; [CALCHAS]'s reading; the army's roar of assent at the end.
> ODYSSEUS: Friends — I do not forget your grief; ten years would shame no man's endurance. But endure LONGER — and remember AULIS! Remember the serpent and the nine! Eight nestlings and the mother, nine — and the god turned it to stone before every eye here! Calchas read it true then and it stands true NOW: NINE YEARS OF WAR — AND IN THE TENTH, THE CITY. We stand IN the tenth. Hold, Achaeans — hold, and take wide-wayed Troy!
> CAPTION: The promise banked at Aulis, princes — I showed you the banking, six songs ago, and told you to wait. Spent here, at the exact hour of despair, precisely as its banker foresaw. The army roared like surf on a headland. The war held. Ledger closed.

### PAGE 19 — SPLASH: THE MUSTER OF THE ACHAEANS.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The full Catalogue as one great image: the entire Achaean army pouring out onto the plain of the Scamander by contingents — bronze fire-bright, the earth itself seeming to groan under them — banners and blazons streaming: the tower shield of Salamis; the boar of Argos; Crete's double axe; Pylos's sands; the black hulls of the Myrmidons NOT marching, their spearmen idle by the ships at the image's margin; and over the host, the dust rising like a mountain mist.
> CAPTION (ornate, top): Then the army poured out to muster as fire pours through a dry forest, and the earth groaned beneath them as it groans under the anger of Zeus. This is the count the great singer needed the Muses themselves to make — for a mortal voice, he says, ten tongues and ten mouths and a heart of bronze could not name them all. The song names every captain and every keel: twenty-nine contingents, forty-four commanders, ELEVEN HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIX SHIPS — the promise of my eighth night's picture, paid in full.
> CAPTION (ornate, bottom): Best of the fighters by far — the song's own judgment — was Ajax, Telamon's son... WHILE ACHILLES RAGED. For the best of all sat apart by his black ships, and his Myrmidons idled along the shore, throwing the discus, tending horses that missed their master's hand. The army marched with its right arm in a sling, princes. Zeus's nod was already working.

### PAGE 20 — Troy comes out.
**PANEL 1.** The wall of Troy: [IRIS] the rainbow-messenger alighting beside [HECTOR] in the likeness of a watchman, her true radiance bleeding through the disguise at the edges. (births refs/iris.png)
> IRIS: Hector — the Achaeans come across the plain in numbers like the leaves and the sands! Bring the army out — and let each captain of the allies marshal his OWN countrymen, tongue by tongue, and lead them himself!
**PANEL 2.** The gates of Troy opening — ALL the gates — the city's whole strength streaming out: Trojans, Dardanians under [AENEAS], the allied tongues each under its own captain: [SARPEDON] and [GLAUCUS] with Lycia at the head of the list's end, as Homer sets them.
> CAPTION: And Hector armed the city, and every gate of Troy opened at once, and the host came out — Trojans and Dardanians and all the tongues of Asia, each nation under its own lord: and last and greatest of the allies, Sarpedon of Lycia, with Glaucus at his side. You know these men, princes. Now watch the plain fill from both edges at once.
**PANEL 3 (wide).** The two hosts advancing toward each other across the whole width of the Scamander plain — the Trojans with CLAMOR like ten thousand cranes, the Achaeans in terrible SILENCE, breathing fury; the dust of both risings joining in the middle air.
> CAPTION: The Trojans came on with shouting and din, like cranes against the morning sky; the Achaeans came on in silence, minded each man to stand by each. And the dust of the two armies rose and met above the plain before the spears did. So — at long last, in the tenth year, in the third book of the master's song — the war stands assembled in one field.

### PAGE 21 — The challenger who stepped on a snake.
**PANEL 1.** Out from the Trojan front: [PARIS-prince] springing into the no-man's ground — leopard skin over his shoulders, curved bow, two spears brandished — challenging ALL the best of the Achaeans to single combat; magnificent.
> CAPTION: And out before the Trojan line sprang — of course — Alexandros: leopard-skin and curls and two bright spears, calling out the best of the Achaeans to fight him man to man. He looked, the song admits, like a god.
**PANEL 2.** And down the Achaean line, seeing him: [MENELAUS] — dismounting from his chariot with the deliberate joy OF A LION FINDING A CARCASS; ten years of a cold hearth arriving at last in one man's face.
> CAPTION: And Menelaus saw him — saw the guest of his hall, the thief of his house — and rejoiced like a starving lion that stumbles on a great carcass. And he came down from his chariot with all his armor, unhurried.
**PANEL 3.** [PARIS], seeing WHO answers: the magnificent challenger shrinking back into the Trojan ranks — the song's exact image: LIKE A MAN WHO STEPS ON A SNAKE in a mountain glen and recoils, trembling, pale.
> CAPTION: And Alexandros saw who had answered — and shrank back into the shelter of the ranks like a man in a mountain hollow who steps on a snake: the spring backward, the trembling, the whiteness in the cheek. In front of both armies. In front of his brother.

### PAGE 22 — Hector's lash, and the terms.
**PANEL 1.** [HECTOR] on his brother, the rebuke like a whip, the whole Trojan front pretending not to hear every word.
> HECTOR: EVIL Paris! Beautiful Paris — woman-mad, honey-tongued cheat! Better you had never been born than stand here now, the mock of enemies! The Achaeans laugh: they thought our champion had COURAGE under those curls. You dared sail the sea, mix with strangers, and carry off a fighting man's wife — a plague to your father, your city, your people, a joy to our enemies — and now you cannot FACE the man you robbed? You would learn what kind of warrior owns the wife you keep! The Trojans are cowards too, brother — else you would have worn a coat of stones* long since for what you have done!
> FOOTNOTE: *A COAT OF STONES — death by public stoning: Hector's own words, and the song's plainest measure of what Troy privately owed its prince.
**PANEL 2.** [PARIS], and give him his due — taking the lash without anger, and rising to the one offer that could redeem the morning.
> PARIS: Hector — the rebuke is just; your heart was always the axe-blade that does not turn. But do not fling golden Aphrodite's gifts in my face; no man chooses the gifts of the gods, and none can refuse them. Now — hear me: set me and Menelaus alone between the armies, for HELEN AND ALL THE TREASURE. Let the winner take the woman and the wealth and lead her home — and let Trojan and Achaean swear friendship, and part: they to Argos, we to our windy city. Let the whole war be two men.
**PANEL 3.** [HECTOR] striding out alone into the space between the armies, holding his spear crosswise, pressing back his own front rank — arrows and stones from the Achaean side falling around him — until [AGAMEMNON]'s great shout stills both hosts to hear the terms.
> CAPTION: And Hector's joy at the offer was a visible thing. He walked out alone between two armies with his spear held sideways, and the Achaeans shot at him until Agamemnon roared them still — and the terms were spoken, and both armies, ten years weary, sat down in two great ranks and laid their armor on the earth, hoping. Hope, princes. Hold your breath with them if you like. But you have read the nod.

### PAGE 23 — The wall: the woman and the elders.
**PANEL 1.** The upper city: [IRIS], in the likeness of a sister-in-law, coming to [HELEN] — and finding her AT THE LOOM, weaving into a great crimson web THE BATTLES OF TROJANS AND ACHAEANS, FOUGHT FOR HER SAKE.
> CAPTION: And Iris went for Helen — and found her where I showed her to you five songs ago: at the great loom, weaving into crimson cloth the struggles of Trojans and Achaeans, endured for her sake, exactly as they happened on the plain below. The web I planted, princes, Homer harvests: she was always the song's first weaver. The Muse merely dictates what Helen already wove.
**PANEL 2.** [HELEN] rising, veiling herself in shimmering white, a sudden sweet longing in her for her first husband, her parents, her city — a single tear — and going quickly to the wall, two handmaids behind.
> CAPTION: And the goddess put in her heart a sudden sweet aching for the man before, and her own people, and her own country — and Helen wrapped herself in pale shining linen and went quickly to the wall, with a tear on her face. Whatever you decided about her four songs ago, princes, the master now complicates it. He will keep complicating it. That is his mercy, and his justice: he never once lets her be simple.
**PANEL 3.** The tower over the Scaean gate: the ELDERS of Troy — too old for war, talkers fine as CICADAS on a tree — watching Helen approach; and their murmured verdict, the most famous whisper in the song.
> ELDERS (murmured): No blame... no blame on Trojan or Achaean, to suffer years of sorrow for such a woman. She is terribly like the deathless goddesses to look upon... And yet — even so — let her go home in the ships, and not be left here, a grief to us and to our children after.
> CAPTION: Both halves, princes. The song gives you both halves in one breath of old men: worth the war — and send her away. Hold both. You will need the practice.

### PAGE 24 — Naming the heroes; the empty places.
**PANEL 1.** [PRIAM] calling Helen to sit beside him with entire gentleness; her answer, wishing she had died before following his son; the old king's absolution.
> PRIAM: Come, dear child, sit by me — you are not to blame in my eyes: THE GODS are to blame, who drove this war upon me. Now tell me: that Achaean there, so royal — head lower than some, but broader in chest and shoulder, pacing the ranks like a great ram through the flock — who is he?
> HELEN: Honored father of my husband — dread to me and dear at once — would that bitter death had pleased me before I followed your son here, leaving my marriage-chamber and my child and the sweet company of friends. But it did not happen so; and I dissolve in weeping for it. That man is wide-ruling Agamemnon: strong king, strong spearman — and my brother-in-law once, shameless thing that I am... if that life was ever real.
**PANEL 2.** The naming continued: Odysseus pointed out — and [ANTENOR], beside them on the tower, adding his own memoir of the embassy: the stiff stance, the downcast eyes — and then the voice, THE WORDS LIKE SNOWFLAKES IN WINTER.
> ANTENOR: I hosted that man once, lady — he and Menelaus, when they came as envoys. Standing, Menelaus over-topped him; seated, Odysseus was the more lordly. And when he rose to speak he stood stock-still, eyes on the ground, staff unmoving — you would have called him a sullen fool. BUT WHEN THE GREAT VOICE CAME, and the words fell like snow falling thick in winter — no man alive could stand against Odysseus then, and we stopped wondering at how he stood.
> CAPTION: Antenor's memoir, princes — I gave you the scene itself two songs ago; here is Homer folding it into the wall-top talk, exactly where he kept it. And huge Ajax was named, and kingly Idomeneus. And then Helen's eyes went searching — and could not find —
**PANEL 3.** THE EMPTY PLACES: [HELEN] scanning the vast host below, face by face, her composure finally failing at one absence.
> HELEN: ...But two I cannot find — two marshals of the people I should see: Castor the horse-breaker and Polydeuces the boxer. MY OWN BROTHERS, born of one mother with me. Did they never sail from Sparta? Or did they sail — and now hold back from the fighting, dreading the shame and the taunts that are MINE to carry?
> CAPTION: So she spoke. BUT THE LIFE-GIVING EARTH ALREADY HELD THEM, THERE IN LACEDAEMON, IN THEIR OWN DEAR COUNTRY. One line, princes. The master answers her question over her head, to you alone, in one line, and moves on — and there is no crueler tenderness in all the songs of men. I promised it to you three nights ago from the winter light. Here it stands. Ledger closed — over her head, forever.

### PAGE 25 — The oath and the old man's mercy.
**PANEL 1.** The plain: [PRIAM] summoned down to seal the truce — the old king and Antenor in the chariot among the armies; lambs brought, wine mixed, water poured over the kings' hands.
> CAPTION: Then Priam himself was called to the plain, for the young might waver but the old king's oath all trusted — read that detail and honor both sides. And lambs were brought, and wine mixed of both armies' pouring, and Agamemnon lifted his hands and swore the terms before Zeus and Sun and Rivers and Earth and the powers under the earth who punish broken oaths.
**PANEL 2.** [AGAMEMNON]'s oath over the lambs (the rite grave and exact, no gore): if Paris slays, Helen and treasure stay; if Menelaus, all returned with recompense — or the war continues to the end.
> AGAMEMNON: Father Zeus, and Sun who sees all things and hears all things — witness! If Alexandros kills Menelaus, Helen and the treasure are his and we sail. If fair-haired Menelaus kills Alexandros, Troy returns the woman and the wealth and pays besides such recompense as men unborn shall hear of. And if Priam's city will not pay — then I remain, and fight to the end of the war.
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM], the oath sealed — climbing back into his chariot and turning it for the city; and his reason, spoken plainly and without shame.
> PRIAM: Bear with me, Trojans and Achaeans: I am going back to windy Ilion. I cannot — I CANNOT — watch with my own eyes my dear son fight the man of war Menelaus. Zeus knows, and the deathless gods know, which of the two goes down to death today. I do not wish to know it before them.
> CAPTION: And no man on either side thought less of him. Fathers, princes. The song never forgets, in ten thousand lines of war, that every man in it is somebody's.

### PAGE 26 — The duel.
**PANEL 1.** The marked ground; lots shaken in a helmet — Paris's leaps out: first cast; the two arming in the sight of both armies; the measured advance.
> CAPTION: The ground was measured, and the lots shaken in a bronze helmet, and Paris's lot leapt out: first throw. And the two armed, and came together in the marked ground between ten years of watching.
**PANEL 2.** The exchange: Paris's spear turned on Menelaus's shield — then [MENELAUS]'s great cast SHEARING through shield and corslet (Paris twisting clear by a hand's breadth) — then the sword-stroke on the helmet-ridge and the sword SHATTERING in three pieces in his hand; Menelaus's roar at heaven.
> MENELAUS: Father Zeus — no god is deadlier to a man than YOU! My spear misses by a finger, my sword breaks like a toy — is the villain to walk away?!
**PANEL 3.** THE DRAGGING: Menelaus seizing [PARIS] by the horsehair crest and DRAGGING him bodily toward the Achaean lines — the chin-strap strangling at Paris's throat — victory one fistful of helmet away.
> CAPTION: And bare-handed he took Paris by the crest and dragged him — dragged the prince of Troy like a carcass toward the Achaean line, the embroidered chin-strap throttling at the soft of the throat. And glory beyond telling was one step from Menelaus — had the strap held. Watch the strap, princes.

### PAGE 27 — The mist, and the defiance of Helen. *(the mandatory scene)*
**PANEL 1.** THE STRAP PARTS — cut by no mortal edge: [APHRODITE] half-manifest over the struggle, one finger drawn through the leather; Menelaus staggering back gripping an EMPTY HELMET; and where Paris was — MIST, and a smell of roses; the goddess spiriting him away to his own fragrant chamber in Troy.
> CAPTION: The strap parted — cut by Aphrodite's finger, easy as a child snapping thread — and Menelaus reeled back clutching an empty helmet, and hurled it wheeling into the Achaean ranks, and turned with his spear for the kill — and there was no one to kill. For the goddess had wrapped her judge in mist and set him down in his own perfumed bedchamber, far from the field. Gods keep their favorites, princes. We have discussed the price of being one.
**PANEL 2.** The wall: [APHRODITE] in the guise of an ANCIENT WOOL-COMBER plucking Helen's fragrant robe — and the disguise failing before Helen's eyes: the perfect throat, the deathless eyes; and HELEN'S DEFIANCE, the bravest words any mortal speaks in the whole poem.
> APHRODITE (as crone): Come. He calls for you — radiant in the chamber, beautiful on the carved bed. You would not say he came from combat; rather from the dance.
> HELEN: ...Goddess. Strange one. WHY must you cheat me further? Will you drive me next to some other favorite's city — some new man of yours in Phrygia — since Menelaus has beaten Alexandros and would take even shameful me home? GO SIT BY HIM YOURSELF. Renounce heaven! Never turn your feet to Olympus again — fret over HIM forever, guard HIM, until he makes you his wife. Or his slave. I WILL NOT GO TO THAT BED. Every woman of Troy would curse my name after — and my heart holds griefs enough.
**PANEL 3.** THE GODDESS UNMASKED IN ANGER: [APHRODITE] blazing to full divinity in the stairwell's shadow, and the threat — naked, total; and Helen's terror, and her obedience: veiling herself in shining white, going silent past the Trojan women, the goddess leading.
> APHRODITE: Do not provoke me, WRETCH — or I shall drop you as extravagantly as I have loved you. I shall set both peoples' hatred grinding against you, Trojan and Greek together — and you will die a death to be talked of. OBEY.
> CAPTION: And Helen, daughter of Zeus, was afraid — and wrapped her shining veil about her and went, in silence, unseen by the women of Troy; and the goddess walked before her. There it is entire, princes: the bravest speech and the most absolute defeat, four songs after I first refused to unweave her for you. Homer refuses too. She fights; she loses; the fighting is real and so is the losing. No singer worth his salt will ever simplify Helen of Sparta. He showed us all how.

### PAGE 28 — The chamber.
**PANEL 1.** The fragrant chamber: the goddess herself — unheard-of — placing a chair for Helen, facing [PARIS] lounging unmarked and beautiful on the bed; and Helen sitting with her eyes turned aside, and the coldest speech a wife gives in the whole song.
> HELEN: Back from the war. Would you had died there — beaten down by the powerful man who was my husband before you. You boasted once you were the better spearman. Go then — call fair-haired Menelaus out again and fight him... No. No, I take it back. Do not. He would kill you at once.
**PANEL 2.** [PARIS], unwounded even in spirit — the shamelessness itself rendered exactly.
> PARIS: Wound me no further, wife. Today Menelaus won — with Athena's help; another day I will win, for we have gods on our side too. Come — no two hearts were ever so taken as mine with you, not even on the first day I carried you from lovely Lacedaemon...
**PANEL 3.** The compulsion settling like weather — restrained, cold, entirely without sweetness: Helen following him from the frame with her face turned from him and from us; and outside the tower window, far below, the plain where a man with an empty helmet still rages.
> CAPTION: And she went with him — the song says it in half a line and shuts the door, and so do I. Downstairs of that closed door, princes, stands everything: a raging honest man, two hoping armies, a sworn truce, and the arithmetic that a duel fairly won has NOT been paid. The woman was to follow the victor. She is following the loser, behind a shut door, at a goddess's compulsion. Something now has to break. Tomorrow you will hear the arrow that breaks it.

### PAGE 29 — The verdict on the plain.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The field: [MENELAUS] stalking the marked ground and the Trojan front LIKE A BEAST up and down — hunting one man; and the Trojan ranks, who owed Paris nothing, unhelpful with a unanimity the song savors.
> CAPTION: And Menelaus ranged the lines like a wild thing, hunting godlike Alexandros. And here is the song's driest verse, princes: NOT ONE MAN of the Trojans or their famous allies could point him out — AND NOT FOR LOVE. Had any man seen him, no man would have hidden him: THEY ALL HATED HIM LIKE BLACK DEATH. The city knew its own accounts. It merely could not stop paying them.
**PANEL 2.** [AGAMEMNON] with the scepter high, proclaiming to both armies the plain arithmetic of the sworn truce.
> AGAMEMNON: Hear me, Trojans, Dardanians, allies! Victory rests with warlike Menelaus — plainly, before every eye. THEREFORE: surrender Argive Helen and the treasure with her, and pay the recompense sworn — such as men unborn shall speak of. Keep the oath!
**PANEL 3.** The Achaean host ROARING assent — and the panel lifting from the plain, up past the walls, past the tower where an old king cannot watch, into the clouds where two unappeased goddesses and a nodded promise are already leaning over the board.
> CAPTION: And all the Achaeans shouted assent, and the terms stood sworn under heaven, and for the space of one held breath the war was OVER — over on lawful terms, princes, with every man alive who was alive that morning. Hold the breath while you can. Above the clouds, Hera has not finished; Athena has not finished; and the nod of Zeus is a debt no truce on earth can pay. Tomorrow: one archer, one arrow, and the breaking of the world's last chance.

### PAGE 30 — The frame: on Helen's stand. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The hall; the NELEID-PRINCE, quiet and shaken.
> NELEID-PRINCE: She told a goddess NO, father. To her face. "Go sit by him yourself — be his slave." No warrior in tonight's song dared half so much... and it bought her nothing at all.
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER.
> SINGER: It bought her the record, child. Three thousand years from tonight — hear me — whenever men weigh Helen of Sparta, that speech will be set in the scale, and it will outweigh armies. The gods can compel the body; the song only asks what the soul did first. Hers fought. Against the one power in heaven that no mortal has ever beaten. Grade her by the enemy, princes — always grade by the enemy.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER, rising, weary and alight at once.
> SINGER: And grade Achilles the same way when the time comes. His enemy will be worse. His enemy will be grief.

### PAGE 31 — The frame closes. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The hall; no one has moved; the fire down to its heart.
> SINGER: Enough. The wrath is lit; the nod is given; Briseis is taken; the duel is won and unpaid; and a truce sworn before Zeus hangs by a thread over the whole plain.
**PANEL 2.** The NELEID-PRINCE.
> NELEID-PRINCE: And tomorrow the thread is cut.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER at the doorway, the stars hard and bright over the sea.
> SINGER: Tomorrow an arrow flies that should never have been loosed, and a good man's blood runs black on a sworn field — and after it, no talk of terms again, ever. Tomorrow the war stops being about Helen, child. Tomorrow it begins to be about itself.

### PAGE 32 — The gnomē. [STYLE-FRAME, ornamented closing panel]
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The empty hall, embers; the stool and the silent phorminx in lamplight; through the doorway the night sea — and lying on the singer's stool, left there, the great scepter-like walking staff of the house, gold-studded, catching the last light.
