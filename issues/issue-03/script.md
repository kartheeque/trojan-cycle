### PAGE 2 — THE TABLET PROLOGUE. [STYLE-TABLET — the archive speaks once, and never again]
**PANEL 1 (full page).** A full-page incised clay tablet in relief registers: upper register — a great mountain empire of fortresses (the east); middle register — a walled city with a sloping rampart on a plain by a strait; lower register — the sea, and oared raider ships with bird-head prows coming from the west. Decorative wedge-bands between registers, no legible script.
> CAPTION (archive voice, top): The records of the kings of the Hatti, the great empire of the east, name a city on the western sea: WILUSA — the city the Greeks call Troy. Its king was bound to the Great King of the Hatti by treaty and oath.
> CAPTION (archive voice, bottom): And the same records name sea-raiders out of the west, from the land the Hatti called AHHIYAWA — the land of the Achaeans. Between the empire and the sea stood Wilusa. So much the clay remembers. The rest is the singer's.
> FOOTNOTE: *HATTI — the great empire of inner Anatolia, east of Troy; its ruler was called the Great King.

### PAGE 6 — The council: between empires. *(Tier H grammar — texture, not plot)*
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-WILUSA-COURT]: [PRIAM] on the low dais; elders and princes; [ANTENOR] with his staff; a SCRIBE reading from a clay tablet held flat on his palms. (births refs/antenor.png)
> CAPTION: Twice a year the tablets came from the east — from the Great King of the Hatti, to whom Troy was bound by old treaty and oath.
> SCRIBE: "...thus speaks the Great King: keep my roads, keep my peace on the shore, and my protection stands over Wilusa as the sky stands over the earth."
**PANEL 2.** [ANTENOR] speaking, measured, finger raised.
> ANTENOR: The oath binds both ways, my king. We keep the Great King's peace; he keeps the spear of the east from our backs. It is a good bargain — while both sides keep it.
**PANEL 3.** A younger noble gesturing angrily toward the sea through the doorway.
> YOUNG NOBLE: And the west, Antenor? Who binds the sea? Ahhiyawan keels were seen off the islands again this spring — the raiders ask no one's leave!

### PAGE 7 — The council: the king's judgment.
**PANEL 1.** [PRIAM] rising from the dais, calm, authoritative.
> PRIAM: Troy has stood between hammer and water since my grandfather's day. Hear the whole of it, and learn how a city lives.
**PANEL 2.** [PRIAM] with one hand toward the east window, one toward the sea.
> PRIAM: To the east — faith. We keep our oath to the Hatti to the last written word, and their strength shadows us. To the west — watchfulness and open harbors. A raider who can trade would rather trade. We fortify the shore, we tax the strait, we feast the stranger who comes in peace.
**PANEL 3.** The council bowing assent; [ANTENOR] satisfied; through the great doorway the peaceful sea.
> CAPTION: So Troy balanced, year upon year, and grew rich on the balance. It was wisely done. Remember, princes, that it was wisely done — for what broke Troy in the end was none of the things its wise men guarded against.

### PAGE 8 — SPLASH: the dream of the firebrand.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The queen's chamber at night: [HECUBA] asleep, heavy with child — and above her sleeping form the dream made visible: a BURNING TORCH rising out of her, and from it fire spreading in a great arc across a dream-image of Troy — walls, towers, ships at the shore, all taken by flame; the dream-fire wheeling around the quiet dark bedchamber. Terrible, beautiful, dignified.
> CAPTION (ornate): In the tenth year of that peace, Hecuba the queen, carrying a child, dreamed a dream: she gave birth not to a child but to a burning torch — and the fire of it took hold of Troy, and the whole city burned to the waterline.

### PAGE 9 — The seers.
**PANEL 1.** Morning: [PRIAM] and [HECUBA], grey-faced from the night, before an old SEER of the gate-shrine who casts marked lots; [ANTENOR] and elders grave behind.
> CAPTION: The dream was told to the seers, and the lots were cast, and the answer came back with one voice.
> SEER: The child the queen carries will be the burning of Troy. If Troy would live... the child must not.
**PANEL 2.** [HECUBA] turned away, hands over her face; [PRIAM] rigid, aged years in a moment.
> CAPTION: What the seers asked, no father's heart could do and no king's heart could refuse. Priam chose as a king — and paid as a father, every day after.
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM], not looking at the herdsman [AGELAUS] who kneels before him, receiving a wrapped bundle from a weeping nurse. (births refs/agelaus.png)
> PRIAM (small): Take him to the high mountain, Agelaus. Do not tell me where. Do not tell me anything, ever.

### PAGE 10 — Ida.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-IDA]: the herdsman [AGELAUS] climbing alone into the high pastures with the small bundle against his chest, pines and mist.
> CAPTION: Agelaus was a kind man given a cruel task. He carried the child five days into the wilds of Ida — and found he could not use the knife. He laid the boy in a sheltered hollow, and fled, and wept as he went.
**PANEL 2.** The hollow among pine roots: the swaddled INFANT — and standing over it, calm and vast, a SHE-BEAR, muzzle lowered gently to the child. No menace: guardianship.
> CAPTION: And the gods, who had their own use for that child, sent a she-bear. Five days she suckled him as her own.
**PANEL 3.** [AGELAUS] returned, staff fallen from his hand, staring at the living child lifting its arms to him; the bear gone; morning light.
> AGELAUS: Alive... Then the gods refuse the seers' price. Come, little one. What heaven keeps, no man may spill. You are my son now — and no one's else.

### PAGE 11 — The herdsman prince.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Years turn: a beautiful YOUTH [PARIS] on the high pasture driving off three rough CATTLE-RAIDERS with staff and sling, cattle safe behind him. (births refs/paris.png)
> CAPTION: The boy grew on the mountain, knowing nothing of Troy, of dreams, of seers. The herdsmen called him Alexandros — "defender of men" — for he beat the raiders from their herds while still half a boy. He was the fairest thing on that mountain, and the mountain knew it.
**PANEL 2.** [PARIS] at ease among the herdsmen at an evening fire, [AGELAUS] watching him with troubled love.
> CAPTION: Agelaus raised him as his own and told him nothing. But some nights the old man looked at the boy's face — a face too fine for any herdsman's son — and was afraid of what he had saved.
**PANEL 3.** [PARIS] alone on a crag at dusk, looking down at the far glitter of Troy on its plain, an unnamable longing on his face.
> CAPTION: And sometimes the boy looked down at the shining city on the plain and felt a thing he had no name for. Blood knows its house, princes, even when the house has barred the door.

### PAGE 12 — The judge of bulls.
**PANEL 1.** A herdsmen's festival on the pasture: a bull-contest ring of stones; [PARIS]'s own great champion BULL garlanded — and facing it a strange WHITE BULL, unmarked, perfect, that no man recognizes; herdsmen arguing.
> CAPTION: At the herdsmen's autumn contest, Alexandros's own bull — his pride, his hand-raised champion — stood against a white bull that no man on Ida had ever seen before.
**PANEL 2.** The two bulls in the ring; the white bull prevailing with unearthly ease; [PARIS] watching, and understanding the ache of what fairness will cost him.
> CAPTION: And the white bull won. It was not close.
**PANEL 3.** [PARIS] placing the victor's garland on the WHITE BULL's horns with his own hands, his beaten champion behind him; the herdsmen murmuring at such fairness; the white bull's eye deep and knowing.
> PARIS: The garland is yours, stranger's bull. My heart wanted otherwise — but the garland is yours.
> CAPTION: He crowned the stranger's bull over his own. Mark that little judgment on a mountain, princes — heaven was watching it. Fairness in a small thing is how the gods choose men for great ones. It is also how they choose their instruments.

### PAGE 14 — Thetis.
**PANEL 1 (tall).** The open sea at dawn: [THETIS] rising luminous from a wave-trough among dolphins, half of the sea and half of light. (births refs/thetis.png)
> CAPTION: In the sea lived Thetis — a goddess, kindest of the daughters of the old sea-god. Zeus himself had desired her. Poseidon had desired her.
**PANEL 2.** [SET-OLYMPUS]: [ZEUS] and beside him [THEMIS], her hand raised in warning — the counsel-pair of the first song.
> THEMIS: Hear the decree that stands over her, lord of gods: THE SON OF THETIS WILL BE GREATER THAN HIS FATHER. Take her, and you raise your own overthrow.
**PANEL 3.** [ZEUS], sober, setting down desire like a cup.
> ZEUS: Then no god may wed her. Let her be given to a mortal man — the worthiest alive. Her son will be the greatest of mortals... and mortal. So the decree is satisfied — and so is my plan. For I will need that son.
> CAPTION: Remember this, princes: the greatest of all heroes will be born not from love, but from heaven's caution.

### PAGE 15 — Peleus.
**PANEL 1.** [PELEUS] at a hillside altar at dawn, alone, making offering with exact care — the most pious of men. (births refs/peleus.png)
> CAPTION: The worthiest mortal alive was Peleus, king of Phthia — not the strongest, not the richest: the most god-fearing. Heaven's choice fell on him, and he was told what he must do to win a goddess: find her on the shore, take hold of her, and not let go.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** Night shore: [PELEUS] wrestling [THETIS] as she SHAPE-SHIFTS in his grip — one panel, many forms flowing: water, white fire, a lioness's forequarters, a serpent coil — his arms locked, his face set, enduring.
> CAPTION: She became water in his arms, and he held. Fire, and he held. Lioness and serpent, and he held. All night he held what no man should be able to hold.
**PANEL 3.** Dawn: [THETIS] in her own form again, standing free; [PELEUS] kneeling, exhausted, head bowed — having won, offering the victory back as reverence.
> THETIS: You held, mortal. And having won me... you kneel?
> PELEUS: A man may hold a goddess, lady. He should not therefore forget what he is holding.

### PAGE 16 — The goddess yields.
**PANEL 1.** [THETIS] looking down at the kneeling king, her immortal face moving from cold to wonder to sorrow.
> CAPTION: And it was the kneeling, not the holding, that won her.
**PANEL 2.** The two walking the dawn shore together, not touching, talking; her sorrow not hidden.
> THETIS: Understand what you take, Peleus. I am deathless. You will age and die. And our son — our son I will love as goddesses love: helplessly, and without power to keep him.
**PANEL 3.** Close: [THETIS] looking out to sea, the grief of all the sea in her face.
> CAPTION: She consented, and heaven rejoiced, and she grieved. Hold her grief in mind, princes. It runs beneath the whole rest of this song like a river under a battlefield.

### PAGE 17 — The wedding prepared.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-PELION]: the centaur's terrace being made ready — nymphs dressing the tables under the plane trees, garlands going up. [CHIRON] directing with quiet joy. (births refs/chiron.png)
> CAPTION: The wedding was set on Mount Pelion, at the cave of Chiron — the wise centaur*, teacher of heroes, gentlest of all the immortal-blooded.
> FOOTNOTE: *CENTAUR — a being half man and half horse. Most were wild; Chiron alone was wise and kind, and princes were sent to him to be taught.
**PANEL 2.** The invitations going out: HERMES flying against the clouds with his herald's staff, a stream of light behind him. (births refs/hermes.png)
> CAPTION: Every god and goddess of heaven was invited by name — the herald of the gods carried the summons himself.
**PANEL 3.** Darkness at the margin of the bright preparations: a gaunt figure in night-dark robes watching from among the pines — [ERIS] — her face bitter. (births refs/eris.png)
> CAPTION: Every god and goddess — except one. Eris, the goddess of Strife, was not summoned. For who invites Strife to a wedding? Learn the answer of this song, princes: Strife needs no invitation. She only needs a door.

**PANEL 1 (wide).** The sky over Pelion at evening: the gods descending in radiance toward the mountain — [ZEUS] and [HERA] foremost, [ATHENA], [APHRODITE], [POSEIDON], [APOLLO], the [MUSE]s in a singing cluster — a river of light bending to the terrace. > CAPTION: The bride was led in veiled in silver. The gods saw her beauty. Only her sisters of the sea saw her eyes.

### PAGE 19 — SPLASH: THE WEDDING ON PELION.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The full wedding feast on the torchlit terrace under the plane trees: gods and goddesses radiant at the boards; [ZEUS] presiding; [PELEUS] and veiled [THETIS] at the head; [CHIRON] standing as host presenting a GREAT ASH SPEAR; [POSEIDON] presenting TWO IMMORTAL HORSES, white as sea-foam; the MUSES singing; nectar going round; the mountain night jeweled with divine light. Joy at full flood — and at the far edge of the torchlight, small, in the outer dark between two pines, a gaunt night-robed figure watching.
> CAPTION (ornate): So Peleus wed Thetis on Pelion, with heaven itself for wedding-guests, and the Muses sang the marriage-song. Men told of that night for a hundred generations: the last feast at which gods and mortals rejoiced together at one table.

### PAGE 20 — The gifts.
**PANEL 1.** [CHIRON] laying the great ASH SPEAR in Peleus's hands — a weapon plainly beyond mortal make.
> CHIRON: Cut from the ash of this mountain, friend, for the son you will have. I have shaped it heavy. He will not find it so.
> CAPTION: Remember the ash spear of Pelion, princes. In all the years of the great war to come, only one man will ever be able to lift it.
**PANEL 2.** [POSEIDON] presenting the two IMMORTAL HORSES, their manes moving like surf.
> CAPTION: And Poseidon gave two deathless horses, Balius and Xanthus, swift as the west wind that sired them. Remember the horses too. A day is coming when one of them will be given a voice — and will weep.
**PANEL 3.** [THETIS] unveiled now at the feast, receiving the gifts meant for her unborn son, her smile and her grief in one face.
> CAPTION: Every gift on that table was for the son to come. And the bride smiled, and thanked the givers, and heard under all the music the one truth she could not drown: the gifts of gods are given to mortals because mortals die.

### PAGE 21 — Strife at the door.
**PANEL 1.** The terrace edge: [ERIS] stepping from the outer dark to the very rim of the torchlight, the feast bright beyond her; in her raised hand, catching all the light at once, A GOLDEN APPLE.
> CAPTION: And at the height of the joy, Strife came to the edge of the light.
**PANEL 2.** Close: the APPLE in her pale fingers, gleaming, perfect.
> CAPTION: She carried one gift of her own: an apple of solid gold. And on it one thing was written — "FOR THE FAIREST."
**PANEL 3.** [ERIS]'s bitter beautiful face, the feast reflected small and golden in her eyes.
> ERIS (small): A wedding-gift... for whichever of you is fairest. Heaven may decide.
> CAPTION: She asked for no seat and no cup. She needed neither. Strife's whole feast is in the throwing.

### PAGE 22 — The apple thrown.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The APPLE in flight over the feast, turning slowly, blazing in the torchlight; below it the upturned faces of the gods.
> CAPTION: She threw it into the middle of the feast — and was gone before it landed.
**PANEL 2.** The apple rolling to a stop on the board — precisely between the places of three goddesses; three jeweled hands reaching for it at once.
> CAPTION: It came to rest — the singer does not think by chance — exactly between three thrones.
**PANEL 3.** The music dead; the MUSES' mouths closed; every god gone still; three goddesses risen to their feet.
> CAPTION: The song stopped. And the last feast of gods and mortals ended — not with a quarrel of men, but with a quarrel of heaven.

### PAGE 23 — Three queens of heaven.
**PANEL 1.** [HERA] with the apple's claim in her bearing, crowned, terrible in majesty.
> HERA: I am the queen of heaven and the wife of its king. The word on this gold names me.
**PANEL 2.** [ATHENA], calm and absolute, spear grounded.
> ATHENA: Wisdom is the highest beauty, and victory the fairest of all things. The word names me.
**PANEL 3.** [APHRODITE], smiling, and her smile itself the whole argument.
> APHRODITE: Sisters. Read the word again — and look at me. The word names me.
> CAPTION: Three goddesses; one word; no shared measure of "fairest." Princes, when a quarrel has no shared measure, it has no peaceful end. Remember this hour when the ships burn.

### PAGE 24 — Zeus refuses.
**PANEL 1.** The three turning as one to [ZEUS]; the whole feast waiting on him.
> HERA: Judge, husband.
> ATHENA: Judge, father.
> APHRODITE: Judge, lord of gods.
**PANEL 2.** [ZEUS], sovereign and unreadable, slowly shaking his head — the wisest refusal in the history of heaven, and the most fateful.
> ZEUS: Not I. Whichever of you I crowned, I would set two immortal angers at my own table forever. This gold needs a judge with nothing to lose that a god would fear to lose.
**PANEL 3.** [ZEUS], looking away east and down — through the night, across the sea, toward a far dark mountain above a sleeping city.
> ZEUS: There is a herdsman on Ida, above Troy. He once crowned a stranger's bull over his own champion, because it was fair. Let the fairest be judged... by the fairest judge among mortals.
> CAPTION: So spoke Zeus. And if you ask whether he knew whose son that herdsman was, and what would follow — ask rather whether the plan of Zeus was ever, for one hour of this song, not walking.

### PAGE 25 — The road to Ida.
**PANEL 1.** [HERMES] receiving the APPLE from Zeus's hand, his quick face for once grave.

**PANEL 2 (wide).** The four crossing the night sky eastward over the dark Aegean — [HERMES] leading, [HERA], [ATHENA], [APHRODITE] behind him, a slow comet of divine light above the black sea.

**PANEL 3.** Below and ahead: moonlit Ida rising above the dark plain, one tiny herdsman's fire on its high shoulder.
> CAPTION: Toward one small fire on a mountainside — and the most dangerous question ever put to a mortal man.

### PAGE 26 — Troy, unknowing.
**PANEL 1.** The palace at Troy, the same night: [PRIAM] and [HECUBA] on the terrace; behind them, grown now, young [HECTOR] — tall, open-faced, honorable. (births refs/hector.png)
> CAPTION: In Troy that night, no one looked at the mountain. Priam's house was full: Hector the eldest, already the best of the young men of Asia; and children beyond counting.
**PANEL 2.** Young [CASSANDRA] at the terrace rail, apart from the others, staring fixedly at Ida, her hands tight on the stone. (births refs/cassandra.png)

**PANEL 3.** Close: [CASSANDRA]'s wide haunted eyes, the tiny reflection of a moving light on the dark mountain in them.
> CASSANDRA (small): Mother... there are torches on Ida. Walking torches. Tell them to put out the torches on Ida...
> CAPTION: No one heard her. No one ever would.

### PAGE 27 — The last tablet. *(Tier H grammar — the irony page)*
**PANEL 1.** [SET-WILUSA-COURT], days later: the routine of empire — the SCRIBE reading a new tablet from Hattusa; [PRIAM] and [ANTENOR] attending; sunlight; order.
> SCRIBE: "...thus speaks the Great King: it is heard that the western sea is quiet this year. Keep it so, and the gods of the oath keep Wilusa."
**PANEL 2.** [PRIAM] and [ANTENOR] exchanging the satisfied look of men whose accounts balance.
> ANTENOR: Quiet to the east, quiet to the west. I have served three kings, my lord, and never known the city safer.
> PRIAM: Then we are watchful in all four directions, old friend.
**PANEL 3.** Through the council-chamber doorway, past their shoulders, far and small: Mount Ida, serene in the summer light.
> CAPTION: In all four directions. But doom was not coming from any of the four directions. It was already inside the walls of the world — on their own mountain, in their own blood, wearing a herdsman's kilt. No treaty in any archive had a clause for that.

### PAGE 28 — The plan, tallied. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
**PANEL 1.** [SET-OLYMPUS], nearly empty — the feast over: [ZEUS] and [THEMIS] alone by the golden loom of the second song.
> THEMIS: Count it with me, lord of gods. The Earth's plea — heard. The instrument of beauty — born, and wed to Sparta. The kings of Greece — bound by their own oath.
**PANEL 2.** [THEMIS], her hand on the shining threads.
> THEMIS: The greatest of heroes — begotten this very season of Peleus and Thetis. Troy — rich, faithful, and owing. And now three goddesses and one golden question... walking up the mountain to the hidden prince.
**PANEL 3.** [ZEUS], looking down through the cloud-floor, the eagle-scepter at rest against his shoulder.
> ZEUS: The board is set, the pieces placed, and every piece was moved by its own will — the seers chose, the father chose, the herdsman will choose. When it is done, no man and no god will be able to say the choice was not theirs. That is the whole art of my plan, Themis. It has no hands. It needs none.

### PAGE 29 — The hinge of the world.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-IDA] at evening: [PARIS] alone with his herds on the high pasture, playing a reed pipe, utterly at peace; the plain and sea below in last light.
> CAPTION: And on the mountain, the herdsman played to his cattle in the evening light, and knew nothing at all.
**PANEL 2.** Behind him, up the darkening path between the pines: a golden radiance beginning, still distant, four points of approaching light.
> CAPTION: He did not know his name was a king's son's name. He did not know a burning torch had been dreamed of him. He did not know that heaven was on his path.
**PANEL 3 (wide, letterbox).** The wide dark mountain; the tiny figure of the piping herdsman; the four lights nearly upon him; Troy glittering far below; the first stars.
> CAPTION: He was, at that hour, the freest and happiest man in this whole long song. Look at him well, princes. The next time the Muse shows him to us, he will be choosing for all mankind.

### PAGE 30 — The frame questions. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The Miletus hall, very late, very still; the SINGER lowering the phorminx; the NELEID-PRINCE with his chin on his fist.
> NELEID-PRINCE: And the herdsman does not know, father. The king does not know. Only the mad girl knows — and no one hears her.
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, grave.

**PANEL 3.** The SINGER's hand flat on the silent strings.
> SINGER: Ask yourself, prince of Miletus — you who will rule men: which voice in your own hall is the one you are not believing?
