# ISSUE 1 — THE BURDEN OF THE EARTH
## The Trojan Cycle, Part I · Script v1
Sources: Tier F (frame); Cypria fr. 1 & Proclus (plan of Zeus); Od. 11 (Tantalus's punishment); attested tradition of the Pelopids; Il. 5, 7, 21 & Pindar Ol. 8 (Laomedon, the walls, Heracles's sack). Lettering conventions (see also the Dialogue Transparency Rule, bible §2B): CAPTION = narrator boxes (ornamented border); SINGER caption = sepia scroll box; dialogue = balloons; a hero "speaking to his heart" = small wavy-edge balloon.

### PAGE 2 — The frame opens. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Exterior, evening: Miletus — an Ionian town on the sea, houses down to the harbor, the megaron hall lit within, stars rising over the Aegean.
> CAPTION: Miletus, on the coast of Asia. Ten generations have passed since the age of heroes ended.
> CAPTION: In the hall of its princes — descendants of Nestor of Pylos — a singer has been summoned.

**PANEL 2.** Interior [SET-MILETUS-HALL]: the hall crowded with nobles; the SINGER led to a carved stool by a boy; the NELEID-PRINCE leaning forward from the high seat.
> NELEID-PRINCE: Sing for us, old father. Sing the great song — our fathers' fathers, and the war at Troy.

**PANEL 3 (close).** The SINGER's face, sightless, serene; firelight on it.
> SINGER: The great song is long, my lords. It does not begin at Troy.
> SINGER: It begins before Helen — before the heroes. It begins with the groaning of the Earth.

### PAGE 3 — The invocation. [STYLE-FRAME → STYLE-MAIN transition]
**PANEL 1.** The SINGER rises, phorminx against his chest, hand lifted; the hall utterly still.
> SINGER: Muse, goddess of memory, sing through me —

**PANEL 2 (tall).** Above and behind him, faint in the rafters' darkness, the [MUSE] appears, radiant, unseen by all — her hand resting toward his brow. The panel's sepia begins to warm into color.
> SINGER: — sing of the great wrath, of the long wanderings, of the burning of holy Wilios*, and of the hard roads home.
> LETTERING NOTE (footnote strip): *Wilios, or Ilios — the city also called Troy; Wilusa in the treaties of the Hatti.
> SINGER: Begin where the gods began it.

**PANEL 3 (wide, full color from here).** The vision opens: the Aegean world from a god's height — sea, islands, smoking towns, ships, tiny wars — teeming, swarming with mankind.
> CAPTION: Hear then, as the Muse gives it: how the plan of Zeus was set in motion.

### PAGE 4 — The burden. [STYLE-MAIN]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Closer over the land: every plain terraced and plowed, every hill walled, herds and armies and harvests crowding the earth; a haze of cookfires.
> CAPTION: In those days the generations of men had grown past all counting. Their kings warred, their cities multiplied, their weight lay heavy on the world.

**PANEL 2.** The land itself seeming to sag and crack under marching armies — and from the cracked hillside, a vast face beginning to rise: [GAIA].
> CAPTION: And the Earth herself — mother of all living things — grew weary.

**PANEL 3 (tall).** [GAIA] risen to her full colossal form against the sky, fields and rivers falling from her shoulders like a robe, her face majestic and exhausted.
> GAIA: Zeus! Hear me! I am the Earth, mother of all living things!

### PAGE 5 — The appeal. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-OLYMPUS]. The gods' hall above the clouds; [ZEUS] enthroned; [GAIA]'s vast form rising beyond the cloud-sea to speak with him, mountain-tall yet suppliant.
> GAIA: The multitude of men is more than I can bear. Their cities crowd my plains; their wars tear my body; there is no rest left in me.

**PANEL 2 (close).** [GAIA]'s face, ancient grief.
> GAIA: I do not ask for their destruction — they are my children. I ask for relief. Lighten my burden, Zeus, or I will break beneath it.

**PANEL 3.** [ZEUS], chin on fist, eyes distant and terrible with thought; the eagle on his scepter seems to watch too.
> CAPTION: And Zeus was silent, weighing the fate of an age in his heart.

### PAGE 6 — The counsel of Zeus and Themis. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
**PANEL 1.** [ZEUS] and [THEMIS] apart on a terrace of Olympus above the cloud-sea, heads bent together — the composition intimate, conspiratorial, cosmic.
> CAPTION: Then Zeus took counsel — not with his queen, nor with the gods at feast, but with Themis, the goddess of Right Order.

**PANEL 2.** [THEMIS], calm, one hand open as if setting weights on an unseen balance.
> THEMIS: Once before, when mankind grew past all bearing, we sent the great flood. Not again — a flood is waste, and it teaches nothing.
> THEMIS: Let it be a war. Let the age of heroes end as it has lived — in glory.

**PANEL 3 (close).** [ZEUS]'s face in profile, resolved; a first flicker of storm in the sky behind.
> ZEUS: Then I will begin it with a quarrel among goddesses — and a judgment by a mortal man — and a woman so beautiful that every king will swear an oath for her sake.
> ZEUS: And at Ilios, by the Hellespont, the finest of the heroes will fall — until Earth's burden is eased, and their names pass into song.

### PAGE 7 — The plan is woven. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** From behind the two gods: far below through parting clouds, two tiny lands picked out in light — the citadel of Mycenae on its rock, and across the sea, Troy on its plain.
> CAPTION: So the plan was made: death for the heroes, and undying glory with it.

**PANEL 2.** [ZEUS] extends his scepter; the eagle takes wing from it, plunging down through the clouds toward the world of men.
> ZEUS: Let it begin far from Troy, and years before it — in the two royal houses that will one day fight the war.
> CAPTION: For the plan of Zeus does not strike like the thunderbolt. It grows slowly, like an oak — from old seed, planted in dark ground.

**PANEL 3 (small, frame-cut).** [STYLE-FRAME] The Miletus hall a heartbeat: the NELEID-PRINCE's hand tight on his cup; the SINGER's voice continuing.
> SINGER: Now hear, princes, of the two old crimes from which your fathers' war grew. One was done at a table. One was done upon a wall.

### PAGE 8 — SPLASH: the two houses.
**PANEL 1 (full page, divided diagonally by the eagle's flight).** Upper left: [TANTALUS] feasting among radiant gods on Olympus. Lower right: [LAOMEDON] proud before the half-built walls of Troy. Zeus's eagle cuts between them.
> CAPTION (top): In the west: the house of Tantalus — whom the gods loved too well.
> CAPTION (bottom): In the east: the house of Laomedon — who cheated the gods of their wage.
> CAPTION (center, ornamented): Two debts owed to the gods. One reckoning to come. From these roots the war will grow.

### PAGE 9 — Tantalus among the gods. [STYLE-MAIN + STYLE-DIVINE]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Olympus at feast: [TANTALUS] the only mortal at the gods' table, seated in honor, gold cup in hand; gods radiant around him.
> CAPTION: No mortal was ever raised so high as Tantalus, king under Mount Sipylos. He sat at the gods' own table; nectar and ambrosia* passed his lips.
> LETTERING NOTE (footnote strip): *Nectar and ambrosia — the drink and food of the gods, which keep them deathless.

**PANEL 2.** [TANTALUS] with his robe gathered, slipping a flask beneath it, eyes sliding sideways; behind him, unnoticed (he thinks), the gods' talk continues.
> CAPTION: But favor bred insolence. He stole the food of deathlessness for his table-companions below —
> TANTALUS (small, to himself): What are secrets among friends?

**PANEL 3.** Down in Sipylos: [TANTALUS] holding court among mortal cronies, pouring the stolen nectar, whispering behind his hand; men laughing.
> CAPTION: — and worse, he carried the counsels of the gods to mortal ears, to make himself great among men.

### PAGE 10 — The abominable feast.
**PANEL 1.** [TANTALUS]'s hall: the gods arrived as his guests — seated, still, unsmiling; covered dishes before them; [TANTALUS] watching with a hidden, testing eagerness. The boy PELOPS is nowhere to be seen. The mood is wrong; shadows too long.
> CAPTION: Tantalus had stolen from the gods and betrayed their secrets — and gone unpunished. So his pride grew, until he believed the deathless gods could be deceived by a mortal's cunning.
> CAPTION: To test them, he did a deed the singer will name only once: he slew his own son, Pelops, and set the flesh before his guests as a feast — to see if they would know.

**PANEL 2.** The gods on their feet as one, terrible; dishes untouched and overturned; divine light flaring; [TANTALUS] shrinking back, arm across his face.
> ZEUS: Abomination! Did you dream we would not know?
> ZEUS: Restore the boy! And for you, Tantalus — a table is prepared in the world below!

**PANEL 3.** Radiance gathered over a bier: the boy PELOPS restored whole and breathing, one shoulder gleaming new ivory; a goddess's veiled figure (Demeter) turning away in sorrow at the panel's edge.
> CAPTION: The gods knew at once, and no dish was touched — save by one goddess, deep in mourning for a lost daughter, who tasted before she saw. The gods restored the boy whole and living. Only that one shoulder could not be made again — and in its place the Fates set gleaming ivory, the mark of his father's sin, carried all his days.

### PAGE 11 — The punishment of Tantalus. (Od. 11)
**PANEL 1 (tall).** The underworld: [TANTALUS] standing to his chin in a clear pool beneath a laden fruit tree — the water shrinking from his straining lips, dark earth showing at his feet; the boughs of pear, pomegranate, and apple whipped just out of reach by wind.
> CAPTION: And in the house of Hades, Tantalus stands forever in water to his chin. He stoops to drink: the pool flees into black earth. He reaches for the boughs: the wind tosses them to the clouds.

**PANEL 2 (small).** Close: his ancient, endlessly thirsting face.
> CAPTION: Food and drink forever before him — and forever beyond his reach.

**PANEL 3.** [STYLE-FRAME] The hall at Miletus: listeners rigid; an old noble making an averting sign; the SINGER implacable.
> SINGER: So ends the first debt, princes. Now for the son, Pelops — and the chariot-race that won a kingdom and a curse.

### PAGE 12 — Pelops comes to Pisa.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Grown [PELOPS] standing on a shore at dawn beside a magnificent chariot with white horses — a gift half-wrapped in sea-mist; the sea behind still churning where a god has withdrawn. Cape and spear; the ivory shoulder catching first light.
> CAPTION: The boy grew into the fairest of men, and Poseidon*, who had loved him, gave him at parting a chariot swift as storm.
> LETTERING NOTE (footnote strip): *Poseidon — god of the sea, of horses, and of earthquakes.
> CAPTION: With it Pelops crossed the sea to woo Hippodamia, daughter of Oenomaus, king of Pisa by the Alpheios.

**PANEL 2.** The gate of Oenomaus's citadel: a row of tall stakes flanking the road, each bearing a bronze-helmed head, weathered to grim silhouette; [PELOPS] driving slowly between them, face set; [HIPPODAMIA] watching from the wall above.
> CAPTION: Thirteen suitors had come before him. Their heads were set on stakes before the gate.
> CAPTION: For an oracle had told Oenomaus he would die by his daughter's husband — and so he raced each suitor to the Isthmus, and speared each one from behind.

**PANEL 3 (close).** [HIPPODAMIA] on the wall, and below, [PELOPS] looking up; their eyes meeting.
> HIPPODAMIA (small, to her heart): Not this one too. O gods, not this one too.

### PAGE 13 — The bargain with Myrtilus.
**PANEL 1.** Night, the stables: [HIPPODAMIA] leading [PELOPS] by torchlight to [MYRTILUS] among the chariots; conspiratorial shadows.
> CAPTION: But Hippodamia went by night to Myrtilus the charioteer, her father's servant, son of Hermes* — the only man whose hands touched the king's axles.
> LETTERING NOTE (footnote strip): *Hermes — god of roads, heralds, and cunning; father of Myrtilus.

**PANEL 2.** [PELOPS] gripping [MYRTILUS]'s forearm, sealing it; [MYRTILUS]'s eyes glittering.
> PELOPS: Take out the bronze pins from the king's wheels and set wax pins in their place. When I am king of Pisa, half the kingdom is yours — I swear it before Zeus, who hears all oaths.
> MYRTILUS: Before Zeus, then, prince. Half the kingdom. The god has heard you — and so have I.

**PANEL 3 (small).** Close: [MYRTILUS]'s hands in lamplight, sliding a wax pin into a wheel-hub, bronze pin palmed.
> CAPTION: So the race was sold before it was run.

### PAGE 14 — The race to the Isthmus.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Full gallop along the coast road: [PELOPS] and [HIPPODAMIA] in the storm-swift chariot ahead; behind, [OENOMAUS] in black armor closing fast, spear already leveled for the killing cast, [MYRTILUS] at his reins.
> CAPTION: They raced for the altar of Poseidon at the Isthmus — the bride in the suitor's car, the father hunting behind with his spear, as twelve times before.
> OENOMAUS: Faster, Myrtilus! The stranger's back makes thirteen — er, FOURTEEN!

**PANEL 2.** The wheel bursts from the king's axle — wax pins shearing; the chariot slewing into ruin at full speed; [MYRTILUS] leaping clear; [OENOMAUS] tangled in the reins, dragged.
> SFX (lettering note): a single jagged impact-line burst, no written sound-word.

**PANEL 3.** [OENOMAUS] broken among the wreck, raising himself on one arm, blood at his mouth, pointing not at Pelops — at [MYRTILUS] standing apart.
> OENOMAUS: Treachery... mine own... Then hear me, gods below — let Myrtilus die... by the hand of the man he served today!
> CAPTION: And with the curse on his lips, the king of Pisa died.

### PAGE 15 — The curse of Myrtilus. SPLASH ELEMENTS.
**PANEL 1.** A sea-cliff at dusk. [PELOPS], now cloaked as a king, and [MYRTILUS] arguing at the brink; [HIPPODAMIA] apart by the chariot, face averted.
> MYRTILUS: My half of the kingdom, son of Tantalus. Pay me. You swore it before Zeus.
> PELOPS: Shall I share my kingdom with the treachery that won it? You are my shame, charioteer — and shame is best drowned.

**PANEL 2 (large, tall).** [PELOPS] hurls [MYRTILUS] from the cliff; the charioteer falling against a bruised sky, arms flung wide, mouth open in the great curse; the sea waiting white below.
> MYRTILUS: Zeus heard your oath, Pelops — now let him hear my curse! As you have betrayed me, let your own family betray one another!
> MYRTILUS: Let your children kill their own blood, and serve one another such feasts, until the gods grow sick of the house of Pelops!

**PANEL 3 (small, bottom strip).** The sea closing over; [PELOPS]'s face half-turned away, the first shadow on it; behind him, unnoticed, faint in the clouds, the veiled suggestion of watching Hermes.
> CAPTION: From that day, men have called that sea the Myrtoan, after Myrtilus. And Hermes, his father, did not forget.
> CAPTION: Remember this cliff, princes. The curse thrown from it will climb back, generation by generation — and it climbs first to the table of Atreus.

### PAGE 16 — Atreus and Thyestes.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-MYCENAE]. The Lion Gate; a herald proclaiming to a crowd; above on the wall, the brothers [ATREUS] and [THYESTES] side by side but not together — a cold gap between them.
> CAPTION: Pelops's sons were Atreus and Thyestes. When the throne of golden Mycenae fell vacant, the city called for a son of Pelops — and the brothers' long hatred began.
> HERALD: Let him reign who can show the gods' own token!

**PANEL 2.** In a torchlit treasury, [THYESTES] holding up a lamb with fleece of solid gold; behind him, half in shadow, a noblewoman (AEROPE, Atreus's wife) who has clearly brought it, her hand still on the casket.
> CAPTION: The token was a lamb with golden fleece, born in Atreus's flocks and hidden away. But Atreus's own wife, Aerope, loved Thyestes in secret — and stole it for him. So Thyestes showed the sign, and claimed the throne.
> THYESTES: Behold the sign, Mycenae! The gods choose ME!

**PANEL 3.** Exterior, dusk — the crowd gasping and pointing west... where the sun is visibly setting in the WRONG quarter of the sky, east, reversing its course; [ATREUS] alone unafraid, arms raised; [THYESTES] staggering.
> CAPTION: Then Zeus answered with his own token, for Atreus: the sun stopped in heaven — and went backward, west to east, against its course. *(caption v2 — clarified causality per review)*
> ATREUS: The heavens themselves un-say you, brother! Mycenae is MINE — and you are banished!

### PAGE 17 — The feast of Thyestes.
**PANEL 1.** Years later: [ATREUS] on the throne receiving [THYESTES], who kneels travel-worn in a suppliant's posture, hands raised to his brother's knees; [ATREUS] smiling with his mouth only.
> CAPTION: In exile Thyestes starved — and in time Atreus learned of Aerope and the stolen lamb — and of the betrayal in his own marriage-bed. Yet he sent word of pardon, and welcome, and a feast of reconciliation.
> ATREUS: Rise, brother. What is past is past. Tonight you dine at my table.

**PANEL 2 (wide).** The feast hall, dark red hangings; [THYESTES] eating heartily at a laden table; [ATREUS] at the head, not eating, watching him with a stillness worse than rage; servants' faces averted; at panel edge a great covered dish, waiting.
> CAPTION: Of what was served at that table, the singer will say only this: Thyestes's young sons had been summoned to the palace before him — and were not at the feast.

**PANEL 3.** [THYESTES] on his feet, the table overturned, his face a mask of absolute horror fixed on the uncovered dish (its contents below panel edge, unseen); [ATREUS] leaning back, savoring; lightning at the windows.
> THYESTES: My sons — MY SONS! Monster! Kin-devourer of your own blood's blood —
> THYESTES: Gods of the dark! Break this house! Let the line of Atreus be served as I was served!

### PAGE 18 — The seed of vengeance.
**PANEL 1.** Night road in storm: [THYESTES] fleeing Mycenae on foot, cloak whipping, the Lion Gate glaring behind him in lightning.
> CAPTION: Twice-cursed now — by Myrtilus's fall and Thyestes's feast — the house of Pelops shone on, golden and rotten at the root.

**PANEL 2.** Years on, a poor hearth in a far country: aging [THYESTES] with his hand on the shoulder of a boy (AEGISTHUS) — teaching him, the boy's eyes already old.
> CAPTION: In exile a son was born to Thyestes: Aegisthus. He was nursed on one lesson only.
> THYESTES: Say it again, my son.
> AEGISTHUS: Remember Mycenae. Remember the feast.

**PANEL 3.** [STYLE-FRAME] Miletus hall: the NELEID-PRINCE pale; the SINGER's blind face lifted.
> NELEID-PRINCE: And was it repaid, father? The feast?
> SINGER: All debts in this song are repaid, child. That one waits for the conqueror of Troy, on the day he comes home... but I run before my horses. Turn we now east — across the sea, to the wall.

### PAGE 19 — Interlude: the map. [STYLE-MAIN]
**PANEL 1 (full page, ornamented border).** A painted map-panel of the Aegean world in Bronze Age style: MYCENAE and PYLOS marked in the west; MILETUS on the eastern shore; and north of it, on its plain by the strait, TROY — with the Hittite lands sprawling vast and dark to the east behind it. Zeus's eagle flies the sea between, its shadow on the water.
> CAPTION: East across the Aegean, at the gate of the Hellespont where all the sea-roads meet, stood Troy — Wilios of the songs, Wilusa in the great king's treaties — westernmost jewel of the empire of the Hatti.
> CAPTION: Her king in the old days was Laomedon. His story is the second root of the war — for the walls of Troy were built by gods... and never paid for.

### PAGE 20 — The gods in servitude.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-TROY-WALLS]. Dawn. Among mortal work-gangs, two laborers who are more than they seem: [POSEIDON-MORTAL] setting a colossal block single-handed while ten men strain at its twin; on the hill beyond, [APOLLO-MORTAL] herding cattle, and where he walks the grass is brighter.
> CAPTION: In that age, for a fault against Zeus, Apollo and Poseidon were bound to serve a mortal man one full year for a wage. The man was Laomedon.
> CAPTION: Poseidon raised the wall. Apollo kept the king's cattle on the ridges of Ida. And the wall they made, no host of men could break.

**PANEL 2.** [LAOMEDON] on a viewing platform, resplendent and pleased, steward beside him with tally-tablets; below, the two disguised gods pausing to look up at him — a long, level look.
> LAOMEDON: Faster, you two! A year runs out, and my wall wants its towers!
> STEWARD (small): The wage for those two, majesty — reckoned at the year's end —
> LAOMEDON (small): The year is not ended.

**PANEL 3.** Detail: one short stretch of wall where the blocks are set by ordinary human hands — good work, but mortal work; beside it, a weathered mortal overseer (AEACUS) wiping his brow, and [POSEIDON-MORTAL] regarding that stretch with a strange, knowing look.
> CAPTION: One short stretch only was built by mortal hands — the work of Aeacus, a pious mortal king who labored beside the gods. Mark that weaker stretch well, princes: the fate of Troy will pass through it. *(v2 — simplified)*

### PAGE 21 — The wage refused.
**PANEL 1.** The year's last day: the two laborers before [LAOMEDON]'s throne, hands out for the wage; the king lounging, his court smirking.
> POSEIDON-MORTAL: The year is served, king of Troy. The wall stands. Our wage.
> LAOMEDON: Wage? For runaway hirelings of whom no city will speak? Here is your wage: be gone unbeaten!

**PANEL 2.** [LAOMEDON] on his feet, pointing, mask of contempt:
> LAOMEDON: And if you stand there glowering — I will bind your hands and feet, crop your ears with bronze, and sell you to the islands for what a mutilated slave will fetch!

**PANEL 3 (tall).** The two gods straightening out of their disguise — not yet full glory, but the room's light bending to them, their shadows suddenly enormous on the walls; courtiers falling back; [LAOMEDON] alone not understanding what he sees.
> APOLLO: Hear him, brother. He has said it with his own mouth, before witnesses.
> POSEIDON: I heard, brother. Let the sea answer him. Let the sun answer him.
> CAPTION: And they were gone from the hall like light off bronze.

### PAGE 22 — Plague and monster.
**PANEL 1.** Troy stricken: streets with smoking pyres, veiled mourners, arrows of light falling slant from a burning sun — Apollo's unseen shafts.
> CAPTION: Then Apollo the far-shooter turned his silver bow on Troy, and plague walked the streets of the unpaid wall.

**PANEL 2 (wide).** The shore: fields drowned in salt flood, and rearing from the waves a KETOS — a vast Bronze Age sea-monster, whale-bulked, serpent-necked, crocodile-jawed — smashing a fishing boat; people fleeing up the beach.
> CAPTION: And Poseidon sent the salt flood, and out of the flood a monster of the deep, to devour the people of the shore.

**PANEL 3.** Temple forecourt: [LAOMEDON] before a smoking altar, priests casting lots and recoiling from the answer; behind him, unnoticed, [HESIONE] going white.
> PRIEST: The gods' price, majesty... is the king's own blood. The monster must have... your daughter. Hesione.
> LAOMEDON: ...Then the gods shall have her. Chain her to the rocks.

### PAGE 23 — Hesione at the rock.
**PANEL 1 (tall).** [HESIONE] chained at the tideline to a sea-rock, gown whipped by wind, chin high and terrified and brave; the sea beyond ominously flat.
> CAPTION: And Troy chained its princess to the rock at low tide — Hesione, who had done no wrong — and the city watched from the walls, and the sea began to move.

**PANEL 2.** On the headland above: a great lion-hooded silhouette against the sky, leaning on a club, a huge bow across his back — [HERACLES] — with [TELAMON] beside him, both looking down at the girl and the stirring sea. Beached below, their ship.
> CAPTION: But that day there put in at the Trojan shore, homing from the country of the Amazons, the mightiest man of that age or any age.
> TELAMON: The sea is standing up, Heracles.
> HERACLES: So it is. And there is a girl in its road.

**PANEL 3.** [LAOMEDON] hurrying down to the shore, all silk and desperation, hands spread to [HERACLES].
> LAOMEDON: Stranger — that club, that bow — save her, and name your price!
> HERACLES: I do not sell rescues, king. But a labor deserves a wage. My price is the divine white mares of your house — the deathless horses Zeus himself once gave to Troy. *(v2 — simplified)*
> LAOMEDON: The divine mares! ...Done, and done, before witnesses!

### PAGE 24 — Heracles and the ketos.
**PANEL 1 (large).** The KETOS erupting from the shallows over [HESIONE]; [HERACLES] already between them, feet planted in the surf, club met against the descending jaws — the classic ACK impact panel, spray sheeting.
**PANEL 2.** [HERACLES] astride the monster's neck, driving his sword home behind the skull; the vast body thrashing a last time.
**PANEL 3.** Quiet after: [HERACLES] snapping Hesione's chain with two fingers; [HESIONE] steadying herself on his forearm, regal even now; [TELAMON] wading in, unable to look away from her.
> HESIONE: My life is yours, stranger.
> HERACLES: Keep it, princess. It was never your debt being paid on that rock.

### PAGE 25 — Cheated twice.
**PANEL 1.** The throne room. [HERACLES] and [TELAMON] before [LAOMEDON]; grooms lead in two ordinary bay horses. The king's face is bland; the court holds its breath.
> LAOMEDON: Your wage, hero: two fine horses of my stable.
> HERACLES: These are not the mares of Tros.
> LAOMEDON: The mares of Tros are Troy's holy things — not fee for a wandering strongman. Take these and my thanks... or take the road.

**PANEL 2 (close).** [HERACLES]'s face: not rage — a terrible calm; behind his eyes the panel faintly ghosts the earlier image of the two cheated gods.
> HERACLES: King of Troy — a god built your wall, and you cheated him. A man saved your child, and you cheat him too.
> HERACLES: Keep the mares. Feed them well. I will come back for my wage — and your wall will not help you, because I know who built it... and I know where it was built by men.

**PANEL 3.** [HERACLES] and [TELAMON] striding down to their ship; [HESIONE] watching from the wall, hand at her throat; [LAOMEDON] laughing with his court, small in the background.
> CAPTION: So Laomedon cheated a second wage — first the gods', now the hero's — and laughed. He would soon learn how long such debts are remembered. *(v2 — simplified)*

### PAGE 26 — The six ships.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Open sea, oars beating: six war-galleys in line abreast, sails full, [HERACLES] at the prow of the first with his bow strung, [TELAMON] beside him with the great body shield.
> CAPTION: Years later, Heracles came back for his wage — not with a great army, but with six ships, picked companions, and a debt to collect. *(v2 — simplified)*

**PANEL 2.** The landing: keels grinding up the Trojan beach, warriors leaping down — boar's-tusk helmets, tower shields, thrusting spears — Trojan horns sounding alarm from the walls beyond.
> HERACLES: To the wall! Not the gate — the WALL! Telamon — you remember the stretch I showed you?
> TELAMON: The man-built stretch, son of Zeus! My father Aeacus laid those stones — I know his work! *(v2 — simplified)*

**PANEL 3.** Under the ramparts: fighting along the shadow of the great sloped wall, arrows sleeting down; [TELAMON] leading a wedge of men with a ram-beam toward one particular stretch.
> CAPTION: For Telamon was the son of Aeacus — the very mortal who had helped the gods build the wall. And a son knows where his father's work is weakest. *(v2 — simplified)*

---

### PAGE 27 — The wall is breached.
**PANEL 1 (tall, dramatic).** The mortal-built stretch bursting inward under the ram; [TELAMON] first through the breach, shield up, roaring, dust and falling block around him — the first man inside god-walled Troy.
> CAPTION: And the wall the gods built was never broken. The wall men built broke — and Telamon of Salamis went through it first of all mankind.

**PANEL 2.** [HERACLES] arriving through the breach a heartbeat later — and stopping dead: his face darkening with terrible jealousy at the sight of another man first in glory, hand tightening on his sword.
> CAPTION: But glory is a dangerous wage too. When Heracles saw another man take the first honor of the breach, the black mood of his line came upon him.

**PANEL 3.** Quick-thinking [TELAMON] on one knee, urgently piling loose stones of the breach into a rough heap — an altar — palms raised over it.
> TELAMON: Stones for an altar! An altar to HERACLES THE GLORIOUS VICTOR — first of heroes, by whose might the wall is down!
> CAPTION: And Heracles stood a moment... and laughed, and the black mood passed — for honor had been given its share. Mark the trick of it, princes: a wise friend can turn a hero's wrath aside. Later in this song you will see what happens when no such friend is near. *(v2 — simplified)*

### PAGE 28 — The fall of Laomedon.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The citadel court in chaos: Trojan spearmen falling back; [LAOMEDON] in armor now, at bay on his own palace steps with his grown sons around him.
> CAPTION: Through the lower city to the palace hill the six ships' men drove them, and at the top there was nowhere left to be carried in a litter.

**PANEL 2.** [HERACLES] drawing his great bow at short range, terrible and unhurried; [LAOMEDON] frozen mid-shout.
> HERACLES: King of Troy. Two wages owed. Payment... in full.
**PANEL 3.** The steps after: fallen king, fallen sons (bodies dignified, ACK convention — no gore, arrows minimal); smoke drifting; one small figure alive among them — the boy [PODARCES], standing over his father's body, a child's knife out, shaking but facing Heracles.
> CAPTION: Laomedon fell, and his sons with him — all his sons but one: Podarces, the youngest, too young for the war and too proud to run.

### PAGE 29 — The price of a veil.
**PANEL 1.** The court, ordered now: captives assembled; [TELAMON] with [HESIONE] beside him — she given to him as his prize of honor; she looking at the boy [PODARCES] among the captives.
> CAPTION: To Telamon, first through the wall, went the first prize of honor: Hesione the princess, whom he had seen at the rock and never ceased seeing.
> HESIONE: Son of Aeacus. My captor and my lord. May a captive ask one gift?

**PANEL 2.** [HESIONE] drawing the veil from her own hair and holding it out over the boy's head — the formal gesture of ransom; [HERACLES] watching, arms folded; [TELAMON] nodding.
> HESIONE: My brother's life. I purchase it — with this veil, and all it is worth.
> HERACLES: Sold, princess. Let all Troy witness: the boy is bought back from the spear.

**PANEL 3 (close).** The boy's solemn face under the held veil.
> HERACLES: Podarces the swift-footed no longer. From this day your name is your ransom, boy: PRIAMOS — "the purchased one." Reign in Troy, and remember what buys and what forfeits: the wage paid, and the wage withheld.

---

### PAGE 30 — The boy king in the ruins.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The six ships pulling away from the smoking shore, [HESIONE] at Telamon's stern rail looking back at Troy until it is small; on the beach, receding, the tiny figure of the crowned boy.
> CAPTION: So Hesione of Troy sailed west to Salamis, a Trojan princess in an Achaean hall. There she bore Telamon a son: Teucer the archer — half-Trojan blood that would one day come BACK to Troy, in the ships of her enemies.

**PANEL 2 (tall).** Amid the rubble of the citadel court, the boy [PRIAM] seated on a fire-scorched throne too large for him, the small gold pendant on his chest, elders of the survivors kneeling; through the broken roof, one shaft of light on him.
> CAPTION: And in the ashes, Troy crowned its child. Priam the purchased rebuilt the walls — and where men's work had broken, men's work was set again, stone by stone, in the same fated stretch.

**PANEL 3 (small).** High above the city, half-formed in the clouds: the faint watching faces of APOLLO and POSEIDON — unappeased, patient.
> CAPTION: But a wall may be rebuilt, and a wage still owed. The gods' grievance was not buried with Laomedon. It waited — for the fullness of the plan of Zeus.

### PAGE 31 — The frame closes. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The Miletus hall, late; the fire low; the SINGER lowering his phorminx; the audience silent, some staring into the embers.
> SINGER: Thus the two roots, princes: the feast in the west, the wall in the east. From the one came Agamemnon and the curse of his house. From the other, Priam and the debt of his city.

**PANEL 2.** The NELEID-PRINCE, quiet:
> NELEID-PRINCE: And the Earth, father? Was her burden eased?
> SINGER: Not yet, child. As yet, not one hero had fallen. The scales of Zeus had only begun to move.

**PANEL 3.** The SINGER's blind face lifted toward the dark doorway and the stars beyond it.
> SINGER: Tomorrow, if the Muse is willing: a wedding on a holy mountain — all the gods among the guests — and one goddess uninvited, with a golden apple in her hand.

### PAGE 32 — The gnomē. [STYLE-FRAME, ornamented closing panel]
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The hall empty but for embers; through the doorway, the star-white Aegean night; the Singer's stool and silent phorminx in lamplight. Large ornamented caption panel, ACK closing-śloka style:
> GNOMĒ: "Two jars stand at the door-sill of Zeus: one holds evils, one holds blessings. To one man he gives of both mingled; to another, sorrow only — and no mortal chooses the ladle."
> CAPTION (small, beneath): — after the words of Achilles to Priam, in the last book of the song of the wrath.
