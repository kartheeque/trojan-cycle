### PAGE 4 — The charge.
**PANEL 1.** [PARIS] fallen back against a pine, terror-struck, arm across his eyes against the radiance; his scattered cattle kneeling, not fleeing.
> CAPTION: Even the cattle knelt. Alexandros the herdsman covered his eyes and waited to die.
**PANEL 2.** [HERMES], kindly, quick, drawing him up by the wrist; the three goddesses waiting in radiance beyond.
> HERMES: Stand up, son of the mountain. You are not to die — you are to judge. Zeus commands it: no god will harm you, whatever you decide. Though I would not envy you either way.
**PANEL 3.** The GOLDEN APPLE placed into Paris's rough herdsman's hands; his face in its glow.
> HERMES: This gold says: FOR THE FAIREST. These three claim it. Zeus refuses to choose. You — the fairest judge among mortals — will give it to one of them.
> CAPTION: So the fairness of a bull-ring came home to roost. Be careful, princes, what you become known for. Heaven takes note — and heaven delegates.

### PAGE 5 — Hera's offer.
**PANEL 1.** [HERA] stepping forward, majesty entire; Paris small before her.
> HERA: Look at me first, herdsman, and hear me. I am the queen of heaven.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** THE VISION SHE OPENS: a dream-panorama unfolding from her raised hand — Paris throned in gold above a hundred cities, kings of all Asia bowing, fleets and armies his.
> HERA: Give me the gold, and I give you lordship: all Asia under your throne, kings for your servants, no city closed to you. Power, herdsman — the first and last of prizes.
**PANEL 3.** Paris staring into the vision, the apple heavy in his hands.
> CAPTION: And the herdsman, who had never owned more than a staff, looked at all the kingdoms of the earth.

### PAGE 6 — Athena's offer.
**PANEL 1.** [ATHENA] stepping forward, calm, grey eyes level; the war-goddess unhurried.
> ATHENA: Now hear me, son of Priam.
> CAPTION: She named his blood before his blood was known. The grey-eyed one wastes nothing — not even a secret.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** THE VISION SHE OPENS: Paris in shining armor breaking enemy lines, always victorious, always unwounded; and beside the battles, Paris grey and wise among elders who hang on his words.
> ATHENA: Give me the gold, and I give you the two things no man keeps long: victory in every battle, and wisdom in every counsel. You will never be beaten and never be a fool.
**PANEL 3.** Paris shaken — this vision tempts a deeper hunger; his hands tighten on the apple.
> CAPTION: This was the better gift. Every wise head in this hall knows it was the better gift. Hold that thought.

### PAGE 7 — Aphrodite's offer.
**PANEL 1.** [APHRODITE] not stepping forward at all — simply smiling; the doves settling; the mountain air gone warm.
> APHRODITE: They offer you thrones and armies, sweet judge. Heavy things. I offer one thing only — and it weighs nothing at all.
**PANEL 2 (wide).** THE VISION SHE OPENS: within a golden mist, a veiled woman of unbearable radiance by a far-off hearth — [HELEN], her beauty rendered as pure light about a half-seen face.
> APHRODITE: The most beautiful woman the sun has ever seen — Helen of Sparta. Give me the gold, and she is yours: your wife, your own, though the whole world stand between you.
**PANEL 3.** Close: Paris's face, undone; the two great visions of power and wisdom already fading behind his eyes.
> CAPTION: She did not mention that Helen was already a wife, already a queen, already the hinge of a sworn oath binding every king in Greece. Gods bargain like merchants, princes: the flaw is never in the listing.

### PAGE 8 — SPLASH: THE JUDGMENT.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The clearing at the still point of the night: [PARIS] placing the GOLDEN APPLE in [APHRODITE]'s open hands, her smile at full triumph; and turning away from the fire into the dark, already leaving, [HERA] and [ATHENA] — their faces not raging but COLD, the cold that outlives rage; [HERMES] watching with the pity of a messenger who has delivered many dooms; the fire small and human at the center of the divine tableau.
> CAPTION (ornate, top): And Alexandros gave the gold to Aphrodite.
> CAPTION (ornate, bottom): Weigh it fairly, princes, before you despise him: he was young, and alone, and it was the goddess of desire herself desiring to win. But weigh also this: in that minute were born the two immortal hatreds — Hera's and Athena's — that will not sleep now until Troy is ash. Not hatred of Paris only. Hatred of Troy, root and branch, wall and cradle. Such is the arithmetic of heaven: one man's choice, one city's bill.

### PAGE 9 — The instruction.
**PANEL 1.** The clearing empty of gods but for [APHRODITE], the apple now a soft glow at her belt; Paris kneeling, changed, frightened by his own fortune.
> APHRODITE: Rise, judge. What I promised, I perform. But a herdsman cannot woo a queen of Greece.
**PANEL 2.** [APHRODITE] lifting his chin with one finger, reading his face like a made thing.
> APHRODITE: First, be known. Go down to the city at the games, and let your blood declare itself. You are no herdsman's son — but that is Troy's tale to tell you, not mine.
**PANEL 3.** She is gone; dawn grey; Paris alone with his cattle and an apple-shaped absence in his hands; far below, Troy.
> CAPTION: And she left him with a promise, a mystery, and a road pointing down the mountain. He took the road. Every step of the rest of this song is that road.

### PAGE 10 — The prize bull. *(Tier C fill — flagged)*
**PANEL 1.** [SET-IDA]: king's men in Trojan livery driving off the pick of the herds — among them Paris's own great CHAMPION BULL; [AGELAUS] protesting, waved aside.
> CAPTION: That season, the king's men came up the mountain to take beasts for the great games at Troy — the games King Priam held every year in memory of a son long dead. They took the best bull on Ida.
**PANEL 2.** [PARIS] arriving too late, staring after the driven bull, then at the road to Troy; decision.
> PARIS: Then I will go to their games — and win him back.
**PANEL 3.** [AGELAUS] gripping the young man's arm, dread naked in his old face.

> CAPTION: The old man knew what he had never told. And he let him go anyway. Ask the fathers in this hall how that is possible. They know.

### PAGE 11 — The games.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The games-field before the walls of Troy: footraces, boxing; a crowd; the princes of Troy competing — [HECTOR] foremost, [DEIPHOBUS] beside him. (births refs/deiphobus.png)
> CAPTION: At the games, an unknown herdsman entered every contest — and won every contest. He outran the princes. He outboxed the princes.
**PANEL 2.** [PARIS] crowned with the victor's wreath before the royal stand — [PRIAM] and [HECUBA] staring at the beautiful stranger with a disquiet neither can name.
> CAPTION: And the king and queen looked at the shining stranger, and something older than memory turned over in both their hearts.
**PANEL 3.** [DEIPHOBUS], shamed and hot, drawing his sword; Paris springing back; the crowd surging.
> DEIPHOBUS: A slave from the hills does not shame the sons of Priam! Your blood will answer it!
> CAPTION: And in one breath the feast became a blade — for the pride of princes is dry tinder. He spoke of blood. He was about to learn whose.

### PAGE 12 — The recognition.
**PANEL 1.** Paris at bay against the altar of Zeus of the Court; Deiphobus advancing; and between them, arms flung wide, wild-haired — [CASSANDRA].
> CASSANDRA: STOP! Strike him and you strike your own house — he is your BROTHER! He is the child of the dream — the son that was cast away!
**PANEL 2.** The stunned field; [AGELAUS] pushing forward, falling to his knees before Priam, holding up a worn INFANT'S TOKEN (a scrap of royal swaddling and a tiny seal).
> AGELAUS: It is truth, my king. I could not do it. The bear nursed him — the gods kept him — I raised him... Forgive an old man. This is your son.
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM] descending from the stand like a man in a dream, taking Paris's face in both hands; [HECUBA] already weeping past them both.
> PRIAM: Alive. Alive. The gods have given back what I paid... My son. My son Alexandros.
> CAPTION: And there was such joy in Troy that day as the city had never known. Hold the joy, princes. Look at it well. It is real joy — and it is the door standing open.

### PAGE 13 — The warning drowned.
**PANEL 1.** The feast of restoration in the palace: Paris in princely robes at last, radiant, between his father and mother; the hall rejoicing; [HECTOR] embracing him with guarded warmth.
> CAPTION: They bathed him and robed him and set him between the king and queen, and the princes embraced their brother — Hector first and most truly, as Hector did all things.
**PANEL 2.** At the hall's edge: [CASSANDRA] again, but now her second cry — pointing at Paris, her voice breaking against the noise of joy.
> CASSANDRA: You are feasting the FIREBRAND! The dream was TRUE — the fire is IN THE HOUSE — put it out, father, put it out while it is still small —!
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM], gentle and immovable, signing for her to be led away; the feast unbroken around them.
> PRIAM: Peace, child, peace. The gods kept him alive against my own decree — the doom is annulled, the debt is paid. Let the house rejoice.
> CAPTION: So the one true voice was led from the hall — kindly, which is worse. The dream was not annulled, princes. It was only postponed. Heaven does not forget; it reschedules.

### PAGE 14 — The frame weighs the joy. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The Miletus hall, unquiet; the NELEID-PRINCE with his hands pressed together.
> NELEID-PRINCE: They forgot the dream, father? A dream that made a king cast out his own son — forgotten in a day?
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, gently.
> SINGER: Not forgotten, child. Overruled. Joy is a strong wine, and relief is a stronger one — and a doom postponed feels exactly like a doom escaped. Exactly like it. There is no feeling in the world more dangerous.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER's face turning grave, the gold light rising.
> SINGER: Now the goddess collects her judge. A ship is building at the shore.

### PAGE 15 — The ship. 
**PANEL 1.** The shore below Troy: a beautiful new ship rising on the stocks, the master-shipwright PHERECLUS at work; [PARIS] watching it grow, [APHRODITE]'s golden radiance faint at his shoulder, seen by no one.
> CAPTION: Phereclus the craftsman built the ships — well-made, fatal ships. The singer of the great war will pause one day, in the middle of the battle, to remember with sorrow the man who built them.
**PANEL 2.** [HELENUS], the seer-prince, and [CASSANDRA] together before [PRIAM]: the twin seers, both with the white band of sight, both grave. (births refs/helenus.png)
> HELENUS: Do not let this fleet sail, father. I have seen its homecoming: it returns with fire in the hold. My sister and I have seen one seeing.
**PANEL 3.** [PRIAM] between his seer-children and his restored son; choosing, again, the joy.
> PRIAM: It is an embassy, Helenus — gifts and greetings to the kings of Greece, no more. Shall I cage the son heaven gave back? Sail, Alexandros — and sail home swiftly.
> CAPTION: Twice warned, princes. Count the warnings as we go. Heaven's ledger does; and the price of each one refused is added to the bill.

### PAGE 16 — The sailing.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The fleet standing out from Troy under sail; [PARIS] at the stem in princely dress; beside him steady [AENEAS] of Dardania. (births refs/aeneas.png)
> CAPTION: So Alexandros sailed for Greece, and with him went his kinsman Aeneas of Dardania — a pious, steady young man, who will outlive everyone in this story. Mark him.
**PANEL 2.** At the rail: [AENEAS] watching Paris, doubt in his steady face.
> AENEAS: An embassy, cousin. Gifts and greetings. Tell me that is all this is.
**PANEL 3.** [PARIS], the apple-light in his eyes, smiling at the horizon.
> PARIS: It is what the goddess wills it to be, Aeneas. I have stopped steering. I sailed the day I judged.
> CAPTION: He spoke truer than he knew. Whether that excuses him, the songs argue to this day — and so will you, princes, before I am done with you.

### PAGE 17 — Sparta receives the stranger.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** [SET-SPARTA]: the Trojan ships in the river-mouth; [MENELAUS] welcoming the embassy at the palace steps with full honors, clasping Paris's wrist.
> CAPTION: And Menelaus king of Sparta received the prince of Troy as guest-law commands: with open gates, with the best of the house, with the wrist-clasp of trust.
> FOOTNOTE: *GUEST-LAW (xenia) — the sacred bond of host and guest, watched over by Zeus himself. To harm one's host — or rob him — was among the deepest crimes the age knew.
**PANEL 2.** The guest-feast: Paris and Aeneas at the high table; gifts exchanged; all correct, all golden.
> CAPTION: Nine days Menelaus feasted him. Gifts were given and received. Every rite of the guest-bond was performed to the letter — by the host.
**PANEL 3.** And across the hall, at the hearth with her women: [HELEN], queen of Sparta, looking up.
> CAPTION: And at the hearth sat the queen.

### PAGE 18 — The goddess at work.
**PANEL 1.** [PARIS] frozen mid-word at the sight of her — the vision of the mountain standing alive by a mortal fire.
> CAPTION: He had seen her once already, in a goddess's golden mist. The mist had not lied.
**PANEL 2.** [HELEN] returning the look for one involuntary moment — then down at her spindle, a line of trouble between her brows.
> CAPTION: And Helen looked back — once — and did not know why. The songs are careful here, princes, and I will be careful with them.
**PANEL 3.** Between and behind the two, visible to no one in the hall: [APHRODITE], faint as heat-shimmer, one hand hovering above each of them.
> CAPTION: For this was not two mortals glancing across a fire. The third figure in that hall was a goddess, and she was working. What Helen chose and what was chosen FOR her, no singer has ever fully unwoven. Homer himself, when he comes to her, will not condemn her. Neither will I.

### PAGE 19 — SPLASH: THE COMPULSION.
**PANEL 1 (full page).** The great hall of Sparta at night, the feast golden below — and rendered above/behind it at divine scale, translucent, [APHRODITE] with her arms spread over the whole room like a weaver over a loom, golden threads of light running from her fingers down to the small bright figures of [PARIS] at the table and [HELEN] at the hearth; [MENELAUS] laughing with his guests, threadless, unaware; the composition making one truth visible: the hall is inside the goddess's hands.
> CAPTION (ornate): This is what the feast truly looked like, if mortal eyes could see: a goddess paying a debt with other people's lives. She had promised the judge the most beautiful woman in the world. That the woman was a wife, a queen, and the guarded treasure of a sworn oath — that was not the goddess's concern. Gods keep their promises, princes. It is one of the most dangerous things about them.

### PAGE 20 — The trust.
**PANEL 1.** A herald with dust on his legs before [MENELAUS]: news from Crete — the funeral of his grandfather Catreus calls him across the sea.
> CAPTION: Then word came that Menelaus's grandsire had died in Crete, and the rites called him — a duty no king could refuse.
**PANEL 2.** [MENELAUS] taking leave of [PARIS] with the full courtesy of guest-law — and to Helen, instructions of trust.
> MENELAUS: Keep the house, my queen, and keep our guests in all honor till I return. [to Paris] The house is yours, prince of Troy — as if I stood in it.
**PANEL 3.** The king's ship going out at dawn; on the shore, watching it go: the guest.
> CAPTION: "As if I stood in it." He gave his trust whole, as the law commands the host to give it. Remember, when the war comes, who kept the law and who broke it. The whole of Greece will remember. They swore to.

### PAGE 21 — The night of the crime.
**PANEL 1.** Night; the hall emptied; [HELEN] alone at the dying hearth — and [APHRODITE] fully present now, seated beside her like a sister, golden, inescapable.
> CAPTION: On that night, the goddess came to the queen — no shimmer now, but seated at her hearth like kin. What was said, no song records. What the goddess is, every song records: she is the power that unmakes resolve. Mortals do not wrestle her and win. Peleus wrestled a sea-nymph; no one wrestles Aphrodite.
**PANEL 2.** The courtyard, deep night: laden Trojan porters carrying CHESTS OF TREASURE from the storerooms to the ships; [AENEAS] standing apart, arms folded, his face dark with shame at what he cannot stop.
> CAPTION: And they did not go empty-handed. The treasure of Menelaus's house went aboard with his queen — the theft sealed to the betrayal, so that no kindness could ever be argued of it. Aeneas kept the ships that night, and kept his silence, and was ashamed. The songs remember that too.
**PANEL 3.** The shore: [HELEN] veiled, pausing once on the gangplank to look back at the dark sleeping palace — her face, in the moonlight, unreadable and wet; behind her, radiant and patient, the goddess; before her, Paris's outstretched hand.
> CAPTION: And Helen of Sparta went aboard the Trojan ship. Compelled, or consenting, or some third thing between that has no name — I told you the songs do not unweave it, and I will not lie to you by pretending they do. She looked back once. Then the ship took her. And somewhere in the dark of Greece, an oath began to wake.

### PAGE 22 — The storm.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** Open sea: the Trojan ships hammered by a sudden black storm, mountainous seas, Paris's ship laboring.
> CAPTION: They were two days out when the sky broke. Hera sent it — her first installment. It scattered the fleet and drove them far off course.
**PANEL 2.** The storm's eye: [HELEN] gripping the mast-stay, drenched; [PARIS] shielding her; above the mast, briefly, a shape of cold majesty in the clouds.
> CAPTION: And in the thunder some swore they saw a crowned shape, watching. Not striking — watching. The queen of heaven is in no hurry. She has a whole war to spend.
**PANEL 3.** Calm after: battered ships limping east under a low sun.
> CAPTION: By strange coasts and long delays — the songs name Sidon of the purple cloth — the ships came at last to the one harbor in the world they should never have entered.

### PAGE 23 — Troy receives the doom.
**PANEL 1 (wide).** The harbor of Troy: the ships coming in; crowds on the shore; [PARIS] leading [HELEN], veiled, down the gangplank; her beauty going through the crowd like wind through wheat.
> CAPTION: So Alexandros brought Helen of Sparta home to Troy. And the city that came to stare fell silent as she passed — and then, being human, it cheered.
**PANEL 2.** The wall above the harbor: [CASSANDRA], both fists against her temples, screaming down at the cheering crowd, unheard in the noise — and beside her [HELENUS], not screaming, simply closing his eyes.
> CASSANDRA: TAKE HER BACK! Ships — I see ships like a forest on the sea — take her back, take her BACK —
> CAPTION: Third warning, princes. The ledger notes it. The crowd cheered louder.
**PANEL 3.** The council chamber: [PRIAM] aged in an hour, [ANTENOR] grim, [HECTOR] with his fists on the table; and the choice already made by the cheering outside.
> ANTENOR: Send her home, my king, with the treasure and an indemnity, and this can still be paid in gold instead of sons.
> PRIAM: ...She has eaten at my hearth, Antenor. She is under my roof and my guest-law now. And done is done. Troy does not hand a suppliant woman back like a strayed heifer.
> CAPTION: Both men were right, by the two laws they lived by — that is the tragedy, and I will not cheapen it. But mark: with those words, one man's theft became the whole city's keeping. The bill was countersigned.

### PAGE 24 — The empty hall.
**PANEL 1.** Sparta: [MENELAUS] returned, standing in his own doorway; the hall dim, the hearth cold, the treasure-room open and bare; a steward kneeling, unable to speak.
> CAPTION: And Menelaus came home from his grandsire's grave to a cold hearth, an emptied treasury, and a gone wife.
**PANEL 2.** Close: [MENELAUS] — not raging; worse: going very still, the honest open face setting like poured bronze.
> CAPTION: He did not shout. Men who shout are asking the world to argue with them. He had nothing left to argue.
**PANEL 3.** A single horseman galloping north through the hill passes at dusk, hooves striking sparks.
> MENELAUS (caption-voice): Ride to Mycenae. Tell my brother. Tell him... the oath.
> CAPTION: Two words, princes. With two words the seed that Odysseus planted at the horse-sacrifice broke the earth at last. Every king who swore now owed his sword. The reckoning of the wedding-court had begun.

### PAGE 25 — The frame pivots: where were her brothers? [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The Miletus hall in uproar of murmurs; the NELEID-PRINCE on his feet.
> NELEID-PRINCE: But father — her BROTHERS! Castor and Polydeuces tore down a fortress when she was a child! Thieves in the night — they would have had her back before the sails were dry!
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, and a long silence; his blind face full of an old sorrow.
> SINGER: Sit, child. You ask the very question Helen will ask herself, years from now, standing on the walls of Troy and searching the Greek host for two faces she will never find. Homer himself will give her that moment. And the answer is the saddest small song inside this great one.
**PANEL 3.** The gold light rising, but colder — a winter light.
> SINGER: While Paris sailed, the sons of the egg fought their last fight. Hear how the Dioscuri ended.

### PAGE 26 — The last raid.
**PANEL 1.** Hill country of the Peloponnese: [CASTOR] and [POLYDEUCES] driving a herd of raided cattle through a defile — and on the ridge above, their cousins and rivals: massive [IDAS] and lean pale-eyed [LYNCEUS]. (births refs/idas.png, refs/lynceus.png)
> CAPTION: There was an old quarrel between the sons of Tyndareus and their cousins of Messene — Idas the strong and Lynceus of the far sight — over cattle, and honor, and the shares of a raid gone sour. That autumn it came to iron.
**PANEL 2.** Night ambush laid: [CASTOR] hidden in the hollow of a great oak, spear ready; the plan visible.
> CAPTION: Castor lay in wait in a hollow oak, as raiders do.
**PANEL 3.** On the far ridge: [LYNCEUS], those terrible pale eyes catching moonlight, seeing THROUGH the oak — his arm flinging out to point.
> CAPTION: But no tree in Greece was proof against the eyes of Lynceus, who could see through oak and earth and stone. He saw. He pointed. And Idas threw.

### PAGE 27 — The death of Castor.
**PANEL 1.** The great spear of [IDAS] transfixing the oak; [CASTOR] fallen from the hollow, hand at the wound, the mortal son of Tyndareus mortally struck. (No gore; the spear in the tree, the fallen man.)
> CAPTION: The spear went through the oak, and through the raider in it. Castor — the horseman, the mortal-born — took his death-wound in a cattle-feud, in the dark, far from any glory.
**PANEL 2.** [POLYDEUCES] arriving like weather: Lynceus down before his rush (dignified, off-panel force — the aftermath, not the blow); and above the fleeing [IDAS], the sky opening — the white fire of a thunderbolt.
> CAPTION: Polydeuces avenged him in the time it takes to say it: Lynceus fell to his hands, and Zeus himself took Idas with the bolt — for the god would not let the slayer of his son's brother see morning.
**PANEL 3.** [POLYDEUCES] kneeling with the dying [CASTOR] in his arms, the immortal cradling the mortal, the cattle forgotten in the defile.
> CASTOR (small, failing): Brother... it is dark early tonight...
> CAPTION: And Polydeuces the deathless held his dying brother, and discovered what immortality is for: it is for outliving.

### PAGE 28 — The shared gift.
**PANEL 1.** The night sky opening above the kneeling brothers: [ZEUS] manifest in majesty, looking down at his immortal son.
> ZEUS: Polydeuces, my son. Your brother was mortal-got; his thread is cut. But you are mine: rise and come — Olympus is open to you, whole and forever.
**PANEL 2.** [POLYDEUCES], not rising; his answer already made; his brother's head against his shoulder.
> POLYDEUCES: Whole and forever — without him? Father, what would I do there? Halve it. Whatever I am, halve it. Give my brother half my heaven and give me half his grave.
**PANEL 3.** The grant made visible: the two brothers rendered as TWIN STARS above the dark hills, rising and setting together.
> CAPTION: And Zeus — who refuses nearly everything — granted it. Day about, the songs say: one day both beneath the earth in Lacedaemon, one day both among the stars. The Twins you steer by, sailors of this hall, are that bargain kept. It is the one wholly honorable thing in tonight's whole song. Naturally, it is also the saddest.

### PAGE 29 — The payoff: the question on the wall.
**PANEL 1.** [SET-SPARTA]: the empty exercise-ground of the Dioscuri, their gear on the wall, dust settling in the light; [TYNDAREUS] an old man alone in the doorway.
> CAPTION: So when the summons of the oath went out through Greece, no ship came from the brothers of Helen. The hearth of Tyndareus had gone dark in a single season: two daughters carried to two dooms, two sons to the earth and the sky.
**PANEL 2.** FLASH-FORWARD, framed in the singer's telling (edges soft, like the visions): [HELEN] years hence on the high wall of Troy, scanning a vast besieging host on the plain below, searching face after face.
> CAPTION: And years from now — Homer has promised it, and I keep his promises — Helen on the wall of Troy will search the whole Greek host for her brothers, and wonder aloud why they alone have not come to fight for her: whether they were ashamed of her. She will get no answer.
**PANEL 3.** The same composition, emptied: only the plain, and in the soil of far Lacedaemon (inset small and quiet), a double grave-mound under two stars.
> CAPTION: For the life-giving earth already held them, there in their own dear country. That is the whole verse, princes, almost as the great singer will make it. I give it to you early, so that when you hear it in its place, you will know why it is the tenderest knife in the Iliad.

### PAGE 30 — The frame questions. [STYLE-FRAME]
**PANEL 1.** The hall, hushed; the NELEID-PRINCE wipes his eyes with the heel of his hand, angry at himself.
> NELEID-PRINCE: A cattle-feud. The rescuers of Aphidna — finished by a cattle-feud while their sister was stolen. Father, is heaven MOCKING us?
**PANEL 2.** The SINGER, shaking his head slowly.
> SINGER: No, child. Heaven is TIMING us. The one sword that could have cut this knot quietly was removed from the board in the same season the knot was tied. Call it chance, and sleep well. Call it the plan of Zeus, and understand the song. I have never been able to tell you which — only that the songs allow both, always both.
**PANEL 3.** The SINGER, rising, weary.
> SINGER: And so nothing now stands between the theft and the reckoning. The oath is waking in every hall in Greece.
